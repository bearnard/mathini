# Grade 6 Geography: doubts (Atlases and global statistics, Coniferous forests)

CAPS source: Social Sciences Gr 4-6, Geography Grade 6 Term 1 "Atlases, global statistics and current events" and Term 3 "Coniferous forests". Grade 6 = real learners: facts the sources disagree on are left out.

## Atlases and global statistics (T1)
- The atlas contents page and index are a MADE-UP sample atlas (pages and block references like "B4"), labelled as a sample. Tests check page counts, that sections follow each other, and that the index is alphabetical.
- Highest mountains: Mount Everest about 8 849 m (8 848,86 m measured in 2020), K2 about 8 611 m, Kangchenjunga about 8 586 m. The three agree across the sources I checked, but they are web reference sites, not a survey agency; please confirm against the atlas the school uses. The lesson tells learners that books may show slightly different figures.
- LEFT OUT on purpose because sources differ: exact river lengths (the Nile and the Amazon are named as the two longest and the lesson says which is first depends on how they are measured), city populations (the order changes with the definition and the year), ocean areas (sources give different numbers; only the ORDER Pacific, Atlantic, Indian is taught), and the third and fourth largest countries (China and the United States depend on whether water is counted).
- "Russia is the largest country, then Canada" and "the Sahara is the biggest hot desert" are standard. Antarctica is the biggest desert of all; the lesson says "biggest HOT desert" and does not list cold deserts.
- Not covered: an exercise of locating current events through the year (CAPS says this goes on all year); the lesson teaches the method only.

## Coniferous forests (T3)
- The climate graph is an EXAMPLE pattern, rounded, not a real town (temperature -12 to 18 °C; rain 25 to 75 mm; 550 mm a year). Real stations vary a lot and the web sources I looked at disagreed with each other, so no town is named. A teacher may want a real station from an atlas to replace it.
- Latitude band 50°N to 65°N is approximate ("roughly"); the boreal belt reaches further in places. Rainforests -10 to 10 and hot deserts 15 to 35 are rounded for the picture.
- "Needle leaves lose little water" and "a sloping shape sheds snow" are standard simple explanations. "Evergreen trees can make food as soon as it is warm enough" is a simplification.
- Animals (wolves, brown bears, moose, lynx, owls) and activities (logging, hunting and herding reindeer, mining, skiing tourism) are typical examples. Please check "herding reindeer" suits the class (it refers to Sámi and other northern peoples without naming them).
- South Africa has plantations of pine but no natural coniferous forest; the lesson says "no natural coniferous forests".
