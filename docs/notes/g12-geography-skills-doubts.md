# Grade 12 Geography, Wave A: geographical skills and techniques, facts for a teacher to check (bron, 2026-11-17)

Source of scope: CAPS FET Geography Gr 10-12 (skills progression table; Grade 12 column). Plan: audit/wave-a/bron-g12-geography-plan.md. The questions carry no picture, so each map situation is described in numbers; no real map sheet, place name or photograph is used.

## Branch A: Map scale, distance and area; Direction, bearings and magnetic declination (T1)

- **Scale conversions** are exact arithmetic: 1 cm at 1:50 000 is 50 000 cm = 500 m. The lesson says "divide by 100 for metres, by 100 000 for kilometres".
- **1:50 000 grid squares.** The lesson states that the grid squares on a 1:50 000 map are 2 cm by 2 cm, so one square is 1 km by 1 km = 1 km². This is how South African 1:50 000 sheets are printed (a 1 km grid), but please check it against a real sheet the learner uses.
- **Curved distance** is taught by adding short straight pieces (or string/paper strip), as the CAPS says "straight and curved".
- **Larger scale.** 1:10 000 is a larger scale than 1:50 000; the lesson says it is 5 times the scale (1 km = 10 cm against 2 cm).
- **Magnetic declination in South Africa is west** of true north. The magnetic bearing is then true bearing + declination, and true = magnetic − declination. **Every exam question gives the declination**; the lessons never assume a value. If a future map shows an east declination, mapwork.ts handles `'E'` but the lessons do not teach it.
- **Declination today.** The worked example follows the usual exam method: years since the map's base year × the mean annual change (in minutes), added to the printed declination, with 60 minutes carried into 1 degree. The figures (24°10' W in 2006, 6' a year) are examples chosen for teaching, not the value for any real map.
- **Bearing arithmetic** (back bearing ± 180°, bearings from east/north offsets using equal offsets only, 045°/135°/225°/315°) is exact. The 16-point names and their bearings (22,5° steps) are standard.
- **Not taught from memory** (Grade 10 revision only): the 1:50 000 sheet numbering system and conventional signs.
