# Grade 9 English HL poetry strand: points for a teacher to confirm

Source: DBE CAPS English Home Language Senior Phase Grades 7-9 (sp_eng_b): poetry (key features; internal structure: figures of speech and imagery, rhyme, rhythm; external structure: lines, stanzas, typography; figurative meaning; mood; analysis of lines, words, stanzas, links and refrain; writing a poem of four to eight stanzas in Grade 9). Four lessons were added beside the umbrella lesson "Poetry and Drama Study". No prescribed or post-1900 poem is used. Every poem is an original poem written for the lesson (labelled) or a public-domain poem with author, title and year; the exact words are asserted in `src/g9Poetry.test.ts`. Bank questions use only short original lines.

## Public-domain texts to check word for word

| # | Poem | Where | What to confirm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | William Wordsworth, "I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud" ("Daffodils") (1807), first stanza | Imagery and symbolism | Wording of the 1807 text ("A host, of golden daffodils;" with the comma); the 1815 version differs in some lines. |
| 2 | William Blake, "The Sick Rose" (1794), complete | Imagery and symbolism | Original capitalisation and punctuation ("O Rose thou art sick!"). |
| 3 | William Shakespeare, "Sonnet 18" (1609), complete (14 lines) | Form and structure | Spelling in the modernised standard text ("dimm'd", "untrimm'd", "ow'st", "grow'st"). |
| 4 | Percy Bysshe Shelley, "Ozymandias" (1818), last six lines | Tone, mood, irony and message | Punctuation of the inscription lines. The first eight lines are not used. |

## Other points

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | Imagery and symbolism | The rose is read as love or beauty, and the worm as something secret that harms it. The lesson says readers suggest different meanings (jealousy, hidden illness, a secret that harms a relationship). | Blake's poem is open to many readings; the lesson stays on the evidence. |
| 6 | Form and structure | The sonnet is described with three quatrains, a couplet and a turn ("volta") at line 9. | Some editors place the turn at line 9, others stress the couplet; the lesson says "many sonnets" have a turn. |
| 7 | Form and structure | Enjambment is shown with the original free-verse poem, not the sonnet, because the sonnet's line ends carry punctuation. | Chosen for a clean example. |
| 8 | Tone, mood, irony | Shelley's "Ozymandias" is read as situational irony and contrast; the tone is called quietly ironic. | Interpretation, not fact. |
| 9 | Analysing and writing | Model answers use 2- and 3-mark wording ("point, evidence, explanation"). | Check against your school's mark scheme. |
| 10 | Analysing and writing | CAPS gives four to eight stanzas for Grade 9 poems (and three to four for Grade 8); the lesson states this. | Check the table in CAPS (creative writing) against your own copy. |
