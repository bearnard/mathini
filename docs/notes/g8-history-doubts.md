# Grade 8 History gaps: points for a teacher to confirm

Source: DBE CAPS Social Sciences Grades 7-9, Grade 8 History Terms 1 to 4 (sp_ss). Branch A adds six lessons to Terms 1 and 2 beside the four term lessons that were already live. Every source box is an original or paraphrased description written for the lesson and labelled as such; no real document, photograph or testimony is quoted. Each source is paired with a "judge the source" or "compare" step, and the lessons give more than one point of view (reformer and mill owner; planter and labourer; owner and worker; African leader and white leaders).

## Points that need a teacher's opinion

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Britain workers | The Grand National Consolidated Trades Union is dated 1833, as CAPS has it. Many books give February 1834 for its founding. | No question uses the date; the lesson says "tried in 1833". |
| 2 | Britain workers | Luddites "1811 to 1816", Swing Riots "1830", Factory Act "1833", Mines Act "1842" (women and young boys banned underground). | Standard; the Factory Act is described as limiting the hours of young children in textile mills, without ages. |
| 3 | Britain workers | No numbers of children, deaths or executions are given. | Estimates vary; left out on purpose. |
| 4 | Britain workers | "Wealth from the slave trade" is a single clause in the last slide. | CAPS lists it; the existing Term 1 lesson also touches it. |
| 5 | Southern Africa 1860 | The map description (Cape and Natal British; Boer republics recognised 1852 and 1854; independent African kingdoms such as the Zulu). | Simplified; other chiefdoms and the Xhosa area are not named. |
| 6 | Southern Africa 1860 | Indenture "usually five years"; first workers "1860"; Passenger Indians "from 1869". | Contract length varied; no wages or numbers are given. |
| 7 | Southern Africa 1860 | The reasons for importing labour follow CAPS (independent Zulu kingdom, demand for sugar, India a British colony). The lesson adds that many Africans could live from their own land. | A common explanation; some historians stress other reasons. |
| 8 | Kimberley | Griqualand West "taken over in 1871"; the claims of Griqua leaders and the Boer republics are mentioned. | Annexation date given by year only. |
| 9 | Kimberley | Black claimholders "pushed out by new rules and white diggers". | Simplified from a longer story of regulations; no dates or numbers. |
| 10 | Kimberley | De Beers Consolidated Mines "1888", "controls most of the supply". | No percentage given, as sources differ. |
| 11 | Labour control | Closed compounds "1880s" (De Beers). | Exact year varies by source; question and lesson use the decade. |
| 12 | Labour control | Xhosa defeat "1878"; Pedi "1879"; Isandlwana "22 January 1879" shown as "January 1879"; Ulundi given as the final battle. | Standard dates; no casualty figures. |
| 13 | Gold | Gold found "1886"; Chamber of Mines "1889". | Standard dates. |
| 14 | Gold | Anti-Indian legislation is described only as "laws limited where Indian traders could live and trade" in the Transvaal. | Law numbers and years are left out. |
| 15 | Gold | Unskilled white workers are mentioned only as demanding protection from cheaper labour. | CAPS lists skilled and unskilled white workers; the detail is kept short. |
| 16 | Turning point | APO 1902; TIC 1903; Bambatha 1906 (tax protest, "put down by force"); Union 1910; SANNC 1912 (later the ANC); Land Act 1913; Satyagraha 1913 to 1914. | Standard dates; no casualty figures for 1906. |
| 17 | Turning point | The Land Act is described as keeping African land ownership to the reserves; no percentages of land are given. | Percentages differ between sources. |
| 18 | All | Names of imagined speakers are not used; sources are described by role (planter, labourer, miner, Randlord, African leader). | Avoids implying a real person said it. |
