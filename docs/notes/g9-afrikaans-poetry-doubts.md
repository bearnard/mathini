# Grade 9 Afrikaans FAL poetry (Wave A): items for a first-language teacher to check

Nothing here has been checked by a first-language speaker. Every Afrikaans word, phrase and sentence shown in a lesson is listed with its English gloss (a test fails if a lesson shows an Afrikaans phrase that is not on this list). All poems are ORIGINAL simple Afrikaans poems written for the lessons and labelled "Oorspronklike gedig, vir hierdie les geskryf"; no published Afrikaans poem is used. Source: DBE CAPS Afrikaans FAL Grades 7-9 (sp_afr_b), poetry strand (key features, figures of speech and imagery, rhyme, rhythm, stanzas, mood, message; own poem: 2 verses in Grade 7, 2-3 in Grade 8, 3-6 in Grade 9). Each lesson has 4 slides and 12 questions. 

## Points that need a teacher's opinion

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Image, symbol, irony | beeld, simbool, ironie, kontras, stemming; the empty chair as a symbol of loss; "Wat 'n lieflike dag" as irony. | Standard school terms; please confirm. The empty chair and the closed door as symbols are common readings, not the only ones. |
| 2 | Image, symbol, irony | Gedig A, B and C (the empty chair, the storm, the shop and the barefoot child) are original lines. | Please check that the Afrikaans reads naturally, for example "die klok tik net aan" and "buite sit 'n kind met kaal voete". |
| 3 | Form and structure | vrye vers, gerymde vers, koeplet, kwatryn, strofe, vers, enjambement | "Koeplet" and "kwatryn" are the Afrikaans forms of couplet and quatrain; "enjambement" is used as a loan term. Please confirm the terms in your textbook. |
| 4 | Form and structure | Rhyme pairs: dag and lag, see and altwee. | Please confirm that both pairs rhyme in standard Afrikaans. |
| 5 | Form and structure | Gedig D is free verse and runs on over three lines (enjambement). | Original poem in the style of the English "Bread" poem; please check the Afrikaans. |
| 6 | Tone, questions, writing | toon, boodskap, hoopvol, grappig, moedeloos, kontekstuele vraag, evalueer, beplan, skryf, hersien | Please confirm natural use, especially "moedeloos" for discouraged. |
| 7 | Tone, questions, writing | Model answers: "Hoopvol (1), want sy lag en sê: Kom in (1)." and an evaluation answer about the word "dood". | Check the mark allocation against your own papers. |
| 8 | Tone, questions, writing | Grade 9 own poem: three to six stanzas (CAPS). | Check the CAPS writing table. |
| 9 | All | Questions are asked in Afrikaans; some options are in English for meaning questions. | House style; please check that no wrong option could be correct in standard Afrikaans. |

## The poems, line by line, with an English meaning

| Poem | Afrikaans lines | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Gedig A | Die leë stoel staan nog by die venster, / asof Oupa nog daar sit en kyk. / Die tee word koud in die koppie, / en die klok tik net aan. | The empty chair still stands by the window, as if Grandpa still sits there looking. The tea gets cold in the cup, and the clock just keeps ticking. |
| Gedig B | "Wat 'n lieflike dag," sê hy in die storm, / terwyl die reën in sy skoene loop. | "What a lovely day," he says in the storm, while the rain runs into his shoes. |
| Gedig C | Die winkel blink met goud en lig, / buite sit 'n kind met kaal voete. | The shop shines with gold and light, outside a child sits with bare feet. |
| Gedig D | My ma knie die deeg / asof sy met dit stry / en wen. /  / Die kombuis vul met warm, / stadige asem. | My mother kneads the dough as if she were arguing with it and winning. The kitchen fills with warm, slow breath. |
| Gedig E | Die son kom op, dit is 'n nuwe dag, / my suster speel en ek lag. / By die strand is net ek en die see, / en die golwe sing vir ons altwee. | The sun comes up, it is a new day, my sister plays and I laugh. At the beach it is just me and the sea, and the waves sing for the two of us. |
| Gedig G | Hulle sê die dorp is dood, / maar by my ouma ruik dit na brood. / Die winkels is toe, die strate is stil, / maar sy lag en sê: "Kom in, as jy wil." | They say the town is dead, but at my gran's it smells of bread. The shops are closed, the streets are quiet, but she laughs and says: "Come in, if you like." |
| Gedig H | Die bus ruik na lemoene en nat jasse, / die ruite is toe van mis. /  / 'n Kind teken 'n hart met sy vinger, / en die dag word skielik anders. /  / Ek klim af in die reën, / maar die hart bly agter op die glas. | The bus smells of oranges and wet coats, the windows are covered with mist. A child draws a heart with a finger, and the day suddenly becomes different. I get off in the rain, but the heart stays behind on the glass. |
