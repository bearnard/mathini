# Grade 8 Natural Sciences: doubts and decisions

## Term 2 (feat/g8-ns-t2)
- Scope follows the Grade 8 CAPS text (read from the official PDF, Term 2, Matter and Materials). Density is qualitative in Grade 8 ("the amount of mass in a given volume"); there is no density formula and no calculation. The one numeric example compares the mass of equal volumes using rounded teaching values (water about 1 g per cm³, cooking oil about 0,9 g per cm³, ice about 0,92 g per cm³, iron about 7,9 g per cm³, wood about 0,6 g per cm³): they are labelled "about" and are not stated as exact. Questions that use them quote the values.
- Gas pressure: only the CAPS idea (more particles in the same container, more collisions, higher pressure). The effect of heating on pressure is stated by the CAPS as not for Grade 8 and is not taught.
- Atoms: the picture shows the first 20 elements with electron shells 2, 8, 8, 2 (the CAPS model task). Neutron numbers and isotopes are Grade 10 and are not taught. Atoms are drawn as a simple model, not to scale.
- Reactions: reactants, products, rearranged atoms and bonds breaking and forming, with word and symbol equations. Balancing is Grade 9 CAPS: the equations shown are already balanced and the learner is only asked to identify reactants and products and to count atoms.
- Solar System is not rebuilt here (the existing lesson is in the wrong term, Wave B).
- Not covered: the practical activities (ball and ring, ether and potassium permanganate diffusion, popit-bead models, electrolysis of copper chloride).

## Term 3 (feat/g8-ns-t3)
- Circuits are QUALITATIVE, as the Grade 8 CAPS says: "the effects of resistance are treated qualitatively by observing the brightness of a bulb in the circuit". There is no V = IR, no equivalent-resistance formula and no unit calculation; a test asserts that no question in the two circuit lessons uses volts, amperes, ohms, V = IR or "calculate". The CAPS statements used: a series circuit has one pathway and the same current everywhere, and every resistor added in series decreases the overall current; a parallel circuit has two or more pathways and the overall current increases when more resistors are added in parallel.
- "Each bulb is dimmer / as bright as one bulb" is the usual classroom result with identical bulbs and a fixed supply. The lesson states it as a comparison, not a measurement.
- Static electricity: learners do not need to memorise which charge a material gets (CAPS note). The lesson says "the material that gains electrons is negative", and the worked example (wool and plastic) is an illustration; no triboelectric series is taught.
- Light: the CAPS text gives the speed of light as 300 000 km per second and the Sun-Earth distance as "49 150 million km", which is a typo; the lesson uses about 150 million km (so the light travel time is 500 s, 8 minutes 20 seconds), a standard round value. Frequencies and wavelengths are named only as highest and lowest (CAPS: no further detail at this level).
- The refraction picture is a schematic (it shows the direction of the bend only); no angles of refraction are given and Snell's law is Grade 11. The reflection picture is drawn from the angle of incidence, and the angle of reflection equals it (tested).
- Not covered: the practical investigations (pinhole camera, steel-wool heating effect, electromagnet, electrolysis, prism) and the history of electricity and the career items (not for examination).

## Term 4 (feat/g8-ns-t4)
- Values follow the Grade 8 CAPS and are quoted in the questions that use them: a light year is about 10 trillion km (the exact value is about 9,46 trillion km, so the CAPS figure is a rounded one), Alpha Centauri is 4,2 light years (42 trillion km), the Earth is about 8 light minutes from the Sun, the Solar System is about 13 light hours across, and the observable Universe is estimated at about 28 billion light years. The lesson says "estimated" for the last one. The check that 8 light minutes is about 150 million km (300 000 km/s × 480 s = 144 million km) is tested.
- Alpha Centauri is described as the brighter Pointer of the Southern Cross, as in the CAPS. The star-map picture is a schematic of the cross and the two Pointers, not an accurate chart. It does not claim that Alpha Centauri is a single star (it is a system of stars); the CAPS treats it as one star at this level.
- The Milky Way picture is a drawn spiral (two arms from a tested spiral function), not a map. The Sun is shown near the outer edge, in an arm.
- Telescopes: optical (lenses refract, mirrors reflect) and radio (a metal dish reflects radio waves), with SALT, Hubble and SKA as examples; the lesson does not go into how an image is formed by the lens or mirror.
- The Solar System lesson (CAPS T4) is not rebuilt: the existing "Solar System and Gravity" is in Term 2 (Wave B list).
- Not covered: the observation tasks (viewing the Southern Cross in September and October), the telescope poster and the careers item (not for assessment).
