# Grade 11 Life Orientation Wave A: teacher-check list

Seven lessons added by `feat/g11-lo-gaps` (14 questions each, 98 in all), from the audit's gaps for Grade 11 Life Orientation: sport and nation building (T1), the media and relationships (T1), study styles and the annual study plan (T2), a healthy balanced lifestyle with accidents and nutrition (T3), risky behaviour, peer pressure and getting help (T3), gender roles and unequal power (T4), religions, beliefs and moral dilemmas (T4). Nothing existing was edited, moved or hidden; the Wave B moves (careers, democracy, study skills, risk and wellbeing) come later. Questions are asked to "you".

## Help numbers (the only four used, checked by a test)

Childline 116; GBV Command Centre 0800 428 428; AIDS Helpline 0800 012 322; SAPS 10111. Please confirm all four are current.

## Sensitive lines to confirm (lowest-risk wording first)

1. **Suicide awareness** (risky behaviour lesson, last slide): only warning signs (talking about feeling hopeless or a burden, pulling away from friends, big changes in mood or behaviour, losing interest in things), what to do (take it seriously, listen, stay with them, tell a trusted adult, school counsellor or clinic, Childline 116, SAPS 10111 if there is immediate danger) and "do not promise secrecy". No method, no numbers, no statistics. The questions only ask about help-seeking.
2. **Substances and sex**: factual and health-focused; consent, the right to say no, protection and testing, clinics. It says alcohol and other drugs are illegal for a young person; confirm. The age of consent is not stated here (it is taught in the Grade 10 lesson).
3. **Gender and abuse of power**: the settings follow the syllabus wording (individual physical abuse, family incest, cultural mourning rules, domestic and sexual violence, sexual harassment at work). The lesson says "sexual abuse by a relative" and does not use the word incest or describe anything. "Women and girls are most often affected" is a general statement; confirm.
4. **Euthanasia**: shown as two sides with no position taken. The lesson says that assisted dying is "currently not lawful" in South Africa; please confirm the present legal position. The balance of the two columns (suffering, dignity, autonomy against the value of life, palliative care, risk of pressure) should be checked.
5. **Religions**: the list (Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Judaism, African traditional religion, Buddhism, non-religious beliefs) is illustrative, not complete; no religion is described in detail and none is ranked.

## Facts to confirm

- **1995 Rugby World Cup** as an example of sport and national unity (the lesson gives only the year and the unifying effect).
- **Study plan**: a 60 mark, 90 minute paper gives 1,5 minutes a mark (arithmetic, but the rule of thumb is a teaching convention).
- **Nutrition**: food groups follow the South African food-based dietary guidelines in general terms; no quantities are given.
- **Study styles** (visual, auditory, kinaesthetic): popular classroom model; some researchers question fixed "learning styles". The lesson says that styles can be combined and that strategy matters most.
- **Peer pressure steps** (say no, short reason, suggest something else, leave, tell someone) are the usual school wording.
