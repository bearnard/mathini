# Grade 8 EMS: doubts and decisions

## Term 1 (feat/g8-ems-t1)
- Scope follows the Grade 8 EMS CAPS Term 1 text: levels of government and their roles (as consumer and producer), government revenue (direct and indirect tax) and expenditure (education, health, housing, social grants, transport, security), the influence of the National Budget on growth and on redressing inequality, standard of living (lifestyles, unemployment, impact of development on the environment), accounting concepts (sole trader, debit, credit, capital, owner's equity, income, expenses, profit, losses, transactions, liability, assets, A = OE + L) and source documents (receipts, deposit slips, till slips, cheques, counterfoils, bank statements, cash invoices).
- VAT is stated as 15% in the examples ("the rate used here") and quoted in every question that uses it, so the lessons do not claim that the rate never changes. The budget example is a small imaginary one (R500 000 revenue and R450 000 expenditure), not national figures.
- The equation example is a five-step service business (capital R20 000; equipment R5 000 on credit; fees R3 000; rent R1 000; R2 000 of the debt repaid). A test recomputes every step by hand and checks that assets = liabilities + owner's equity each time. Drawings are not in the Grade 8 list and are not taught.
- Debit and credit are named as the two sides of an account; double entry and T accounts are Grade 8 Term 4 and come in a later lesson.
- Deposit totals use the notes R200, R100, R50, R20 and R10; coins are not used. Cheque counterfoils are described as the record kept in the cheque book.
- Not built: the Term 1 formal tasks (the data response and the controlled test).

## Term 2 (feat/g8-ems-t2)
- One small service business, "Thandi's Tutoring", is used for the cycle, the cash journals, the ledger and the statements (seven cash transactions: capital R10 000, fees R4 500 and R2 500, rent R1 500, telephone R400, salaries R1 200, equipment R2 000). A test recomputes the journals, bank balance, ledger, trial balance, income statement and balance sheet by hand and checks that every total balances and that the Bank column of the CRJ equals the sum of its analysis columns. No liabilities arise (cash transactions only), so the balance sheet is assets = owner's equity.
- Scope follows the Grade 8 CAPS Term 2: the overview of the accounting cycle (transactions, source documents, subsidiary journals, General Ledger, Trial Balance, Income Statement, Balance Sheet), the Cash Receipts Journal of a service business (columns and uses, source documents, entering, closing off, effect on the accounting equation) and factors of production and markets. Posting from the journals to the ledger in detail (T accounts, double entry) is CAPS Term 4 and is shown here only as a result (the trial balance).
- The CRJ columns used are Date, Document, Details and Bank, with the analysis shown as Capital and Fees earned. Real school formats vary (some add Sundry or Interest columns); the lesson says so.
- Factors of production follow the CAPS wording: capital (own and borrowed), labour (unskilled, semi-skilled, skilled), natural resources, entrepreneurship and remuneration. Fair employment practices are listed in general terms (contract, safe workplace, at least the minimum wage, no unfair discrimination); no minimum-wage figure is quoted because it changes. The wage bill example (5 workers at R200 for 20 days) is imaginary.
- Markets: the goods and services market and the factor market (labour and financial markets). The circular flow is a simple picture, not a full model.
- Not built: the Term 2 formal tasks (the project and the mid-year examination).

## Term 3 (feat/g8-ems-t3)
- The Cash Payments Journal and the combined journals use the same business as Term 2 (Thandi's Tutoring): receipts R17 000, payments R5 100, cash balance R11 900 carried forward. Tests recompute the Bank column against the analysis columns, the balance, and the accounting equation after all seven transactions (receipts raise assets and equity; the three expenses lower both by R3 100; the equipment purchase swaps one asset for another). Assets = owner's equity = R13 900, with no liabilities (cash transactions only).
- CPJ columns used: Date, Document and details, Bank, with the analysis shown as Rent, Telephone, Salaries and Equipment. School formats vary.
- A small display fix is included: the Term 2 CRJ table now also uses three columns (Date, Doc and details, Bank), because four columns were clipped at 320 px width.
- Forms of ownership are at the characteristics level the CAPS asks for: sole trader, partnership (profit shared in an agreed ratio, e.g. R60 000 in 3 : 2 gives R36 000 and R24 000), close corporation, private company ((Pty) Ltd) and public company (Ltd), limited liability, advantages and disadvantages, and the role in sustainable job creation and sustainable use of natural resources. No legal detail (no section numbers, member or partner limits) is given. The close-corporation note is stated as: no new close corporations can be registered since 1 May 2011 and existing ones continue, as in the Grade 12 lesson.
- The statements about advantages and disadvantages are general; the best form depends on size, capital and risk, which the lesson says.
- Not built: the Term 3 formal tasks (the case study and the controlled test).

## Term 4 (feat/g8-ems-t4)
- Management follows the Grade 8 CAPS Term 4 text: the levels (top, middle, operational or supervisory), the four tasks (planning, organising, leading, controlling), characteristics of good management and the three styles (autocratic, laissez-faire or permissive or free-rein, democratic or participatory). The advantages and disadvantages of each style are general classroom statements; the lesson says that the best style depends on the situation. The only number is a controlling example (planned R10 000, actual R8 500: R1 500 below the plan), computed and tested.
- The General Ledger lesson uses the same Thandi's Tutoring books as Terms 2 and 3. A test recomputes the double entry by hand: seven postings, total debits = total credits = R22 100 (R17 000 of receipts and R5 100 of payments, each counted once on each side), the Bank account (debits R17 000, credits R5 100, balance R11 900 debit) and the trial balance (debit balances R11 900, R2 000, R1 500, R400 and R1 200; credit balances R10 000 and R7 000; both columns R17 000).
- The CAPS item "sections within the General Ledger" is taught as the Balance Sheet accounts and the Nominal (income and expense) accounts. Some textbooks name more sections (for example separate debtors and creditors sections); those belong to Grade 9 (credit transactions) and are not taught here.
- Opening accounts: the lesson says an account is opened when its first transaction appears; real books open all the accounts at the start of the period. A trial balance only shows that debits equal credits, which the lesson says.
- Exam preparation (study techniques, writing skills; CAPS weeks 7 to 9) is not taught as a lesson.
