# Grade 7 Afrikaans FAL poetry (Wave A): items for a first-language teacher to check

Nothing here has been checked by a first-language speaker. Every Afrikaans word, phrase and sentence shown in a lesson is listed with its English gloss (a test fails if a lesson shows an Afrikaans phrase that is not on this list). All poems are ORIGINAL simple Afrikaans poems written for the lessons and labelled "Oorspronklike gedig, vir hierdie les geskryf"; no published Afrikaans poem is used. Source: DBE CAPS Afrikaans FAL Grades 7-9 (sp_afr_b), poetry strand (key features, figures of speech and imagery, rhyme, rhythm, stanzas, mood, message; own poem: 2 verses in Grade 7, 2-3 in Grade 8, 3-6 in Grade 9). Each lesson has 4 slides and 12 questions. 

## Points that need a teacher's opinion

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Figures of speech | vergelyking (soos), metafoor (is), personifikasie, beeldspraak | Standard school terms; please confirm they match the terms your textbook uses. |
| 2 | Figures of speech | Gedig A: Die son is 'n goue appel in die lug, / die wind fluister saggies in die gras, / en my hart is so lig soos 'n veer. | Original poem with one metaphor, one personification and one simile; please check the Afrikaans reads naturally. |
| 3 | Figures of speech | Bank sentences such as "Sy oë blink soos sterre", "Die nag is 'n swart kombers", "Die gras is 'n groen see". | Each uses one figure only; "Die gras is groen" is the non-figurative example. |
| 4 | Sound and structure | rym, rymskema (ABAB, AABB), alliterasie, herhaling, vers, reël, refrein | Standard; the Afrikaans spelling of the plural "reëls" and "verse" is used. |
| 5 | Sound and structure | Rhyming pairs: hoog and droog, strand and sand, huis and muis, son and kon, kat and mat, hond and mond. | Please confirm all pairs rhyme in standard Afrikaans pronunciation (hoog and droog, son and kon). |
| 6 | Sound and structure | Gedig D uses a refrain "ek wag en ek wag vir die lente". | Simple, regular Afrikaans. |
| 7 | All | Questions are asked in Afrikaans; some meaning questions give the options in English. | House style; please check that no wrong option could be correct in standard Afrikaans. |

## The poems, line by line, with an English meaning

| Poem | Afrikaans lines | English meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Gedig A | Die son is 'n goue appel in die lug, / die wind fluister saggies in die gras, / en my hart is so lig soos 'n veer. | The sun is a golden apple in the sky, the wind whispers softly in the grass, and my heart is as light as a feather. |
| Gedig B | Die dag is warm, die son is hoog, / die kinders speel op die strand. / Die see is blou, die lug is droog, / en golwe loop oor die sand. | The day is warm, the sun is high, the children play on the beach. The sea is blue, the sky is dry, and waves run over the sand. |
| Gedig C | Die slang sluip saggies oor die sand, / sy skubbe blink in die son. | The snake slides softly over the sand, its scales shine in the sun. |
| Gedig D | Die winter is koud en die dae is kort, / ek wag en ek wag vir die lente. / Die vure brand laag, die wind is wild, / ek wag en ek wag vir die lente. | The winter is cold and the days are short, I wait and I wait for spring. The fires burn low, the wind is wild, I wait and I wait for spring. |
