# Grade 6 Life Skills (Personal and Social Well-being) additions (Wave A): points for a teacher to confirm

Source: DBE CAPS Life Skills Grades 4-6, Grade 6 Personal and Social Well-being, Terms 2 to 4. Written for 11 to 12 year olds. The existing 12 umbrella lessons are untouched.

## Term 2: bullying and cultural rites of passage

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Bullying | Four kinds (physical, words, leaving out and rumours, online), reasons, where to find help (trusted adult, teacher, counsellor, Childline 116, free), not hitting back, how to stop. It is stated that the child who is bullied is never to blame. | Childline's number is 116 (free); the school's own counsellor and reporting rules differ, so a teacher should add them. |
| 2 | Bullying | Nothing graphic and no names of real children. | By design. |
| 3 | Rites of passage | Birth, baptism, wedding and death are described generally, with the meaning, personal and social significance of each. Only two examples are named: Christian baptism and the Xhosa welcoming ceremony imbeleko ("many Xhosa families"), plus "other families welcome a baby in their own way". | A teacher or cultural reviewer should confirm that the examples and wording are respectful and accurate, and may wish to add examples from the cultures in the class. |
| 4 | Rites of passage | No ritual is described in detail, and the lesson says that families differ in how, when and whether they hold a ceremony. | By design. |
| 5 | Rites of passage | Baptism is called "the Christian ceremony in which a child is welcomed into the church". | Some denominations baptise older children or adults; the lesson says "many Christian families". |

## Not covered on purpose
- Detail of rituals and of the customs of particular cultures beyond the two named examples.

## Term 3: caring for animals, caring for people, nation-building and cultural heritage

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Animals | Cruelty is shown as hurting, neglect, abandoning, chaining in the sun and making animals fight; care as food, water, shelter, exercise and a vet; places of safety as the SPCA, shelters and wildlife centres. The lesson says that cruelty to animals is against the law (the Animals Protection Act). | General statements; a teacher may add the local SPCA. |
| 7 | Animals | Children are told to tell a trusted adult and not to deal with cruelty or a strange animal alone. | By design. |
| 8 | People | The "I feel... when... I need..." sentence is taught for saying needs kindly. | A common approach, not CAPS wording. |
| 9 | Nation-building | National days and symbols are in `psw6Facts.ts` and tested: Human Rights Day 21 March, Freedom Day 27 April, Africa Day 25 May, Mandela Day 18 July (67 minutes), Women's Day 9 August, Heritage Day 24 September, Reconciliation Day 16 December; flag first flown on 27 April 1994 (six colours); the anthem joins Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika and Die Stem and is sung in five languages; coat of arms of 2000 with the motto !ke e: /xarra //ke ("Diverse people unite"). | Standard facts. **Children's Day** is in the CAPS list but has no fixed public-holiday date, so no date is given. Africa Day is not a public holiday in South Africa. |
| 10 | Nation-building | "Eleven official languages" is used. | Standard. |


## Term 4: basic first aid, food hygiene, communicable diseases (HIGHEST RISK: a nurse or first-aid teacher should read the first-aid table)

Only simple, widely taught steps for 11 to 12 year olds. No medicine name or dose appears anywhere (a test fails on them). Every lesson tells the child to get an adult and call for help first. Emergency numbers are in `psw6Facts.ts` and tested (ambulance 10177, 112 from a cell phone, Poisons Information Helpline 0861 555 777).

| # | Lesson | What it says | Why it is uncertain / who to ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | First aid | Stay safe, shout for an adult, call 10177 (112 from a cell phone); say where you are and what happened. | Standard. |
| 12 | First aid | Small cut: rinse with clean water, cover with a clean plaster. Bleeding: press firmly with a clean cloth, add a second cloth if soaked and keep pressing, get an adult; heavy bleeding means emergency help; use a cloth and wash hands afterwards. | Standard St John / Red Cross advice. |
| 13 | First aid | Bruise: a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for "a short time" (no minutes given). | Deliberately vague. |
| 14 | First aid | Burn or scald: cool under cool running water for at least 10 minutes (HEALTH.burnCoolMinutes), cover loosely with a clean cloth, get help if big, deep or on the face. Never butter, toothpaste or ice. | Some guides say 20 minutes; "at least 10" is true for both. |
| 15 | First aid | Sunburn: out of the sun, cool wet cloth, drink water; prevention hat, sunscreen, shade "in the middle of the day". | No hours given on purpose. |
| 16 | First aid | Bee sting: an adult scrapes the sting out, cool cloth. Serious allergy (swollen face, trouble breathing): 10177 at once. | Standard. |
| 17 | First aid | Snake bite: keep the person still and calm, do not cut or suck, get to a hospital. | Standard South African advice; no bandage or tourniquet is described. |
| 18 | First aid | Dog bite: wash with soap and water, tell an adult. | Standard; rabies is not mentioned. |
| 19 | First aid | Poisoning: tell an adult, keep the container, phone the Poisons Information Helpline 0861 555 777 or 10177; never make the person vomit. | Standard. Confirm the helpline number is still current. |
| 20 | First aid | Choking: if the person can cough, let them cough; if they cannot cough, speak or breathe, shout for an adult and call 10177. The lesson does NOT teach back blows or the Heimlich manoeuvre to children. | By design: a first-aid teacher may want to add them. |
| 21 | Food hygiene | Wash hands with soap and water for about 20 seconds (HEALTH.handWashSeconds); separate board for raw meat; cook chicken right through; fridge for milk, meat and leftovers; "soon after the meal", no hours given. | Standard (WHO / Department of Health). |
| 22 | Food hygiene | Typhoid and cholera named as serious diseases spread by dirty water or food. | True; South Africa has had outbreaks. No numbers given. |
| 23 | Food hygiene | The lesson says to throw away the whole loaf when there is mould. | Standard advice for bread. |
| 24 | Diseases | Colds, mumps and chickenpox are caused by viruses; TB by bacteria; athlete's foot by a fungus. | Standard. |
| 25 | Diseases | A cough lasting more than 2 weeks is a reason to go to the clinic (HEALTH.coughWeeks). TB is treated with medicine from the clinic for "many months" (no figure). | Standard South African public-health message. |
| 26 | Diseases | "Anyone can get TB" and people on treatment deserve kindness; HIV is not mentioned. | By design; the CAPS links TB and HIV only in later grades. |
| 27 | Diseases | Mumps: a vaccine from the clinic helps to prevent it; chickenpox: stay at home until the spots dry up. | Standard; no vaccination schedule is given. |
| 28 | Diseases | Athlete's foot: "a clinic, a doctor or a pharmacist can give a cream". No product name. | Standard. |
