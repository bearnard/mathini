# Grade 11 Economics gaps: doubts and decisions

## Population and labour force (T1), economic growth (T2), marginal utility (T2)
- CAPS (Grade 11 Economics, Term 1 weeks 1-2): "Population and labour force: characteristics, importance, remuneration" (with the factors of production). Term 2 weeks 1-2 lists "Marginal utility" under price elasticity, and Term 2 weeks 7-8 lists the wealth creation process and economic growth: meaning and importance, methods, constraints on growth, South Africa's recent growth experience, and the standard of living.
- All data are imaginary teaching data and are given in each question; no Stats SA figure is quoted, because the figures change every quarter. The imaginary town has 1 000 thousand people: 300 children, 600 of working age (300 employed, 100 unemployed, 200 not economically active) and 100 elderly. Rates: participation 66,7%, unemployment 25%, absorption 50%, dependency ratio 66,7. A test recomputes each rate by hand and checks that employed + unemployed + not active = working-age.
- Definitions follow the official (strict) Stats SA definitions in outline: working-age 15 to 64; the unemployed are without work, available and looking; discouraged work-seekers are not counted as unemployed in the strict definition. The expanded definition is not taught.
- Growth: growth rate = (later − earlier) ÷ earlier × 100; real GDP = nominal GDP ÷ price index × 100; per-capita growth compares GDP and population growth. South Africa's recent growth is described only as "low and uneven" with the usual constraints (electricity, skills, unemployment); no figure for any year is quoted.
- Marginal utility: schedules in imaginary utils; TU is the running total of MU, maximal where MU = 0. Consumer equilibrium uses MU per rand; a test searches all possible purchases with R8 for bread (R2) and milk (R4) and confirms that 2 loaves and 1 litre of milk give the most utility and that MU per rand is 6 for both. Indifference curves are not part of the CAPS item.
