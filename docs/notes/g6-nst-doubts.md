# Grade 6 Natural Sciences and Technology: doubts and teacher-check list

CAPS source: Intermediate Phase NST Gr 4-6, Grade 6 Terms 1 and 2 ("Food processing", "Dissolving", "Mixtures and water resources", "Processes to purify water").

## Teacher check (safety and health wording)
1. BOILING WATER (Purifying Dirty Water, slide 2, bank question on boiling): the lesson says an adult heats the water until it bubbles strongly and keeps it boiling "for about a minute", then lets it cool with a lid on. Please confirm the wording with a health teacher. Children are told an adult does this; no child handles a kettle or a stove.
2. CLASSROOM WATER: the lesson and a bank question say that water cleaned in a class experiment must NOT be drunk (CAPS says learners should not drink it).
3. Water-borne illness: only "diarrhoea" is named, with no detail about symptoms. The safe-behaviour steps are: do not drink water from a river, dam or puddle; use clean tap water or water an adult has boiled or treated; wash hands with soap after the toilet.
4. Chemicals in water: the lesson names chlorine and "purification tablets" only as things that kill germs; no amounts and no instructions for children to use them.

## Content doubts
- Food processing: CAPS names the reasons (edible, last longer, improve nutrients) and a list of methods. Examples are mine: pap, bread, frozen peas, dried fruit, biltong, salted fish, pickled cucumbers, maas (amasi) and mageu. Please check the indigenous examples are suitable (the lesson avoids beer).
- "Freezing only stops germs from growing" and "salt pulls water out" are simplified.
- Fermenting "improves nutrients" is CAPS wording; the lesson does not claim more.
- Nutrient loss: "long boiling in lots of water washes vitamins out" is general guidance; no percentages are given.
- Wetland picture: the flows are in made-up "units" with a simple store-and-release model (half of the water present leaves each step). It is an illustration of "acts like a sponge", not data from a real wetland. Water is conserved in the model (tested).
- Sand filter layers (fine sand 40%, coarse sand 30%, gravel 30%) are an example for the sums; real filters vary, but fine on top and coarse below is correct.
- Not done in this branch: "Rates of dissolving" (branch B), the Mains electricity gaps (branch B) and Geography (branch C). The design-make-evaluate water-cleaning project is not turned into a lesson.
