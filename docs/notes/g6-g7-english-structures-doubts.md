# Grades 6 and 7 English HL language structures: doubts and decisions (for an English teacher to check)

Orchestrator #1040/#1041. Each example sentence was written so that exactly one option is correct; anything debatable is listed here and kept out of the questions.

## Grade 6 (branch feat/eng6-structures-a): T1 pronouns, subject-verb agreement and simple tenses, prepositions; T2 prefixes, suffixes and stems, continuous tenses and the present perfect
- Task ids start with `eng6-` (never `gr6-`): the weekly top-up treats `gr6-*` as Mathematics. The task instructions are in their own file, `eng-gr6-structures.json`.
- Scope follows the Grade 6 English HL teaching plans (word level, sentence level, spelling and punctuation columns): interrogative, demonstrative and indefinite pronouns (T1 and T3); subject-verb agreement and the simple tenses (T1); prepositions (T1); stems, prefixes and suffixes and the dictionary (T2); the continuous tenses and the present perfect (T2).
- Pronouns: whom is listed as the formal interrogative for a person as the object, and no question depends on it. Demonstrative pronouns are taught only when they stand alone (This is my seat); when they come before a noun (this seat) they are determiners, and no question uses that form. Everyone, someone and nothing take a singular verb. Few and many are listed as indefinite pronouns, following the CAPS wording.
- Prepositions: between for two and among for more than two is taught as the rule; some speakers use among and between more loosely, so only clear cases are asked. Time: at with clock times, on with days and dates, in with months, years and parts of the day. The sentence The school day starts at 08:00 has the time preposition at; by 08:00 would also be correct English but means no later than, so it is not an option.
- Agreement: the verb to be is shown with I am, he is, we are; a subject joined by and is plural. The irregular past forms taught are went, ate and saw.
- Prefixes and suffixes: un (not), re (again), dis (opposite), mis (wrongly), pre (before); ful (full of), less (without), ness, ly, er (a person who), ment. Misspell is used to show that the stem keeps its spelling. Word division: between syllables with a hyphen (beau-ti-ful); the lesson also says never to leave a single letter on a line, which is a common school rule rather than a CAPS rule.
- Tenses: the present perfect is taught as has or have plus the past participle, with the participles finished, eaten, gone, seen and written. The past continuous question uses when I phoned, which shows an action in progress in the past. Present perfect continuous appears only as a wrong option and is not taught.
- Spelling: South African English throughout (colour, practise as a verb, practice as a noun; neither word is needed in these lessons).
- The words o'clock and 08:00 are used for time; no decimal numbers appear.
- Not built: the Term 3 and Term 4 structures (past perfect and future perfect, active and passive voice, direct and indirect speech, adverbs, punctuation) which are in branch feat/eng6-structures-b.

## Grade 6 Terms 3 and 4 (branch feat/eng6-structures-b): active and passive voice, direct and indirect speech, past and future perfect, adverbs, punctuation
- Task ids start with `eng6-` and the instructions are in `eng-gr6-structures.json`. Five lessons: T3 Active and Passive Voice, Direct and Indirect Speech, Past Perfect and Future Perfect; T4 Adverbs, Punctuation (commas, colons, semi-colons, contractions and brackets).
- Direct and indirect speech: the lesson teaches the usual school rule, that after a past reporting verb the verb moves back one tense, pronouns change, tomorrow becomes the next day and here becomes there. In real English a speaker may keep the present tense when the statement is still true (She said that she is tired). The questions that depend on the rule say so in the prompt, so exactly one option is correct. Reported questions and commands are not taught.
- Quotation marks are shown as double marks ("...") and the full stop, question mark or exclamation mark goes inside the closing marks. Some books use single marks; the lesson mentions inverted commas as the other name.
- Passive voice: taught with a form of be plus the past participle, keeping the tense of the active verb; the doer is added with by. The questions ask for the passive or active form of a given sentence with the same tense. The perfect and continuous passives (has been marked) appear only as wrong options.
- Past perfect and future perfect: had plus the past participle, and will have plus the past participle, with the participles seen, gone, eaten, finished and written. Would have (conditional) is not taught, and was avoided as an option where it could be argued.
- Adverbs: manner, time, place, degree, duration and frequency, with the position rule for frequency adverbs (before the main verb, after the verb to be). Words such as downstairs and outside are treated as adverbs of place. Never is an adverb of frequency.
- Punctuation: commas in a list without a comma before and, a comma after an introductory word such as however, colon before a list, semi-colon between two related main clauses, apostrophe in the contractions don't, she'll, can't, it's, and its (no apostrophe) for belonging, brackets for extra information. The lesson says in the mistake that a comma before the last item is not used in this lesson; some teachers accept a comma there.
- Spelling: South African English.

## Grade 7 Term 1 (branch feat/eng7-structures-a): adjectives and comparison, prefixes, suffixes and roots, sentence parts and clauses, words with more than one meaning, spelling and the dictionary
- Task ids are `gr7-<name>-t1`; the instructions are in `eng-gr7-structures.json`. Scope follows the Grade 7 English HL Term 1 columns: nouns and adjectives with degrees of comparison; prefixes, suffixes and roots; subject and predicate, main and dependent clause; homophones, homonymy and polysemy; dictionary usage, spelling patterns and rules.
- Comparison: more and most with long adjectives, er and est with short ones, doubling and y-to-i rules, irregular good, bad and many. No question uses an adjective where both forms are acceptable (for example common, commoner or more common; clever, cleverer or more clever are avoided as answers).
- Roots: port (carry), dict (say), scrib or script (write) and aud (hear) only. Roots of other words (for example ject) are not taught. Prefixes in, im and non are taught as meaning not; before p the prefix is im.
- Clauses: a clause is taught as a group of words with its own subject and verb; a phrase has no subject and verb of its own. The main and dependent clause examples all start with because, when, although, if or that. Relative, noun, adjectival and adverbial clauses are Term 3 and are in a later branch.
- Homophones and homonyms: homophones are taught as words that sound the same but are spelt differently, and homonyms as words with the same spelling and sound and unrelated meanings (bat, bank, bark, match). Some dictionaries call all of these homonyms, and polysemy (related meanings, such as head) is defined in the lesson but no question asks the learner to tell polysemy from homonymy, because the boundary is debatable.
- Spelling: South African English (colour, favourite, centre). Practise is the verb and practice is the noun. The licence and license pair is not used. The plural rules taught are s, es, consonant plus y to ies, f or fe to ves and irregular nouns; the rule for nouns ending in o (potatoes, photos) is not taught.
- Dictionary: headword, part of speech (n., v., adj.), meaning, example and guide words.
- Not built here: Term 2 to Term 4 structures, which are in the later G7 branches.

## Grade 7 Term 2 (branch feat/eng7-structures-b): active and passive voice, prepositions and adverbs, direct and reported speech, apostrophe, hyphen and quotation marks
- Task ids are `gr7-<name>-t2` in `eng-gr7-structures.json`. Scope follows the Grade 7 English HL Term 2 columns: adverbs, prepositions, active and passive voice, hyphen and apostrophe; passive, present progressive, direct and reported speech, transitive and intransitive verbs; quotation marks.
- Passive: only transitive verbs; the progressive passive (is being repaired) is taught for the present only. Good at, not good in, is used for the fixed pair; some South African speakers also say good in maths, so the option good in is not used in any question.
- Reported speech: the usual school rule (the verb moves back one tense, am to was, can to could, will to would; now to then, tomorrow to the next day, yesterday to the day before, this to that, here to there). The questions that need the rule say so. Reported questions use statement word order and a full stop, with if for a yes or no question; reported commands use told or asked and to. Say, tell and ask are shown with their usual patterns only.
- Hyphen: numbers from twenty-one to ninety-nine, compound adjectives before a noun, a few prefixes and word division between syllables. Some style guides write some compounds without a hyphen; only clear cases are asked.
- Apostrophe: singular owner (girl's), plural owner ending in s (girls'), plural owner not ending in s (children's), its for belonging, it's for it is. The names of the apostrophe uses (possession, contraction) are taught, but the possessive of a name that ends in s (James's or James') is not taught because both are accepted.
- Quotation marks: double marks, the end punctuation inside the closing marks. Single marks are also used in some books and in British newspapers; the lesson says nothing about them.
- Hard and hardly, late and lately are taught as different words; lately is taught only with the meaning recently.
