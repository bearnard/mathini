# Grade 9 isiXhosa FAL: teacher-review pack for the transactional texts

Status: **PACK FOR A FIRST-LANGUAGE TEACHER. No isiXhosa lesson or question is shipped from this pack.** Agreed in #889 and #894. A learner will see isiXhosa text only after a first-language teacher has written or approved it.

## Why no lessons are written from this

The Afrikaans lessons (Wave A) are short and regular, and every Afrikaans sentence is on a teacher-check list. I cannot give the same assurance for isiXhosa: correct concords, conjunctive writing (for example `Ndingu Litha` or `NdinguLitha`), tense forms, honorifics (izihlonipho) and spelling are exactly the points the Grade 6 isiXhosa audit already found uncertain (`audit/teacher-check.md`, sections A and B). A wrong sentence taught to a second-language learner is harder to correct than a missing lesson, so the content must be written by a first-language teacher. What I can do is prepare everything around the writing: what CAPS requires, the structure of each text, the English skeleton, the questions to be written, and a checklist.

## What is missing (CAPS, Grade 9 isiXhosa uLwimi lokuQala oloNgezelelweyo, Ibanga 7-9, Ibanga le-9 Ikota 1 to 4)

Source: `sp_xho_b` (the DBE CAPS isiXhosa FAL Senior Phase), the Grade 9 term tables ("Ukubhala nokunikezela") and the text-type table "IINTLOBO ZEETEKISI EZIBHALWAYO". Findings from `audit/caps-alignment.md` (Grade 9 isiXhosa FAL).

| CAPS term | Text type (CAPS wording where it is in the CAPS) | Our lesson today |
|---|---|---|
| Ikota 2 | Ungeniso kwidayari (diary entry); rules / constitution / policy; agenda and minutes (imizuzu); notice | generic "Writing & Presenting, T2" only |
| Ikota 3 | will and testimony (umyolelo; discussion on ilifa, estates and wills); formal letter with a CV (isivi) | generic "Writing & Presenting, T3" |
| Ikota 4 | letter of application; i-imeyile (e-mail); diary; obituary; invitation card | generic "Writing & Presenting, T4" |

The CAPS text-type table itself describes two of these in isiXhosa, which a reviewer can use as the authority:

- **I-imeyile**: "Yenzelwa ukwazisa ngokuthile nokugcina ubudlelwane" (for informing and keeping up a relationship); the parts are the recipient's address, **Abakotshelwa (CC)**, **Isihloko** (a summary of the content), **Umyalezo**, **Igama lomthumeli**; the sender's address appears by itself, and the extra details at the end are called **lutyikityo (isignitsha)**.
- **Ungeniso kwidayari**: "Idayari kukubonisa amava akho buqu"; a review of the day or event, first person where it fits, simple and direct language, the tone depends on what is written.
- **Isivi**: the CV is built for a particular post and shows what suits the advertised post.

## Six lessons proposed (the same set as English HL and Afrikaans FAL), once a reviewer has written the content

| # | Term | Proposed lesson | What the learner must be able to do | English skeleton to be turned into isiXhosa |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2 | Ungeniso kwidayari | open an entry with the date, say what happened (past tense), say how they feel, close with a plan | date line; "Dear Diary"-style opening if natural; 3 sentences about the day; 1 sentence about a feeling; 1 sentence about tomorrow |
| 2 | 2 | Umgaqo-siseko / imigaqo (constitution and policy) of a club | name the parts and write clauses with a duty and a permission | name; aim; membership; meetings; amendments; date adopted; signature; one policy of one matter (a cellphone policy) |
| 3 | 3 | Umyolelo (will) | key words (testator, heir, executor, estate, witnesses), the parts and order, exact sentences | title and name; executor; two bequests (a house, an amount in rand); date; signatures of the testator and witnesses |
| 4 | 3 | Isivi nencwadi eshicilelwa nayo (CV and covering letter) | CV headings, referees, a short covering letter that names the post | personal details; qualifications; experience; referees; covering letter: post, why suitable, CV attached, available for an interview |
| 5 | 4 | Incwadi yesicelo somsebenzi (letter of application) | layout, opening, body, ending, formal register | two addresses, date, formal greeting, subject line, three short paragraphs, formal closing |
| 6 | 4 | I-imeyile (e-mail) | parts (to, CC, subject, message, signature), formal and informal e-mail | a formal e-mail asking for the school hall; an informal e-mail to a friend |

The house style would match the Afrikaans lessons: five or six slides, each with a rule, a worked example, a common mistake and one picture; at most 130 words a slide; isiXhosa in one style with an English gloss; **12 multiple-choice questions** a lesson (72 in all).

## What the first-language reviewer is asked to write or check

For every row of the table above, please supply (in isiXhosa, with an English gloss) or approve:

1. The **key words** the learner needs (about 8 per lesson) and the heading names a Grade 9 teacher would use, in the spelling used in schools (conjunctive writing).
2. **Four to six model sentences** per lesson, in the form a second-language learner can reproduce: present, past and future where the text type needs them, with the correct subject concords, tense markers and (where relevant) honorifics.
3. A **model text** of about 60 words for lessons 1, 3, 4 and 6 (the others can be a layout with headings).
4. **Common mistakes** a learner makes in each text type (two per lesson): wrong concord, spacing of the copula, wrong register, wrong tense.
5. For the 12 questions per lesson: the stems may be English; the isiXhosa answers and distractors must be real, plausible isiXhosa, and exactly one must be right.
6. A decision on points where usage varies: the written form of the copula with names, regional forms, the closing of a formal letter, and the isiXhosa word for "constitution of a club" (CAPS gives `Umgaqo-siseko` for the national Constitution; a club constitution may be `Umthetho-siseko` or `Imigaqo` in schools).

### Return format (what makes the next step quick)

A table per lesson with these columns: `isiXhosa | English | register (formal/informal) | tense | reviewer note`. Every row becomes a line in the lesson and in `audit/teacher-check.md`; a test (as for Afrikaans) will fail if a lesson shows an isiXhosa phrase that is not in the returned table.
