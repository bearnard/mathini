# Grade 8 isiXhosa FAL: teacher-review pack for the transactional texts

Status: **PACK FOR A FIRST-LANGUAGE TEACHER. No isiXhosa lesson or question is shipped from this pack, and none is written in it.** Ordered in #971 (same approach as the Grade 9 pack, `docs/notes/g9-isixhosa-review-pack.md`). A learner will see new isiXhosa text only after a first-language teacher has written or approved it.

## Why no lessons are written from this

Same reason as Grade 9. Concords, conjunctive writing (`NdinguLitha` as the current Grade 8 lessons write it, or `Ndingu Litha`), tense forms, honorifics and spelling are the points the existing isiXhosa audits already flag (`audit/xhosa-g8-teacher-notes.md`, `audit/teacher-check.md`). A wrong sentence taught to a second-language learner is harder to undo than a missing lesson. So every isiXhosa sentence in this pack is **either quoted from the CAPS (marked "CAPS") or absent**. Where a skeleton needs isiXhosa, it says "to be written by a first-language teacher" and gives the English meaning only.

### The CAPS text-type table as authority

The CAPS itself describes several of these in isiXhosa. A reviewer can treat these as the standard wording:

- **Ileta yobuhlobo / engekho sesikweni**: one address (the writer's), the date written below it, a correct greeting after the address, introduction, body, ending; no fixed rule for the ending, but it is followed by the writer's first name; language from informal to formal; written for people one loves (friends, family) to express feeling (congratulating, sympathising, advising).
- **Ileta esesikweni / eya kumhleli wephepha-ndaba** (the table lists the letter to the editor as one kind of formal letter): two addresses (writer and recipient), a suitable greeting after the recipient's address, a **subject line** (`umqolo wesihloko`) underlined after the greeting, introduction, body, ending; the closing is followed by the writer's initials and surname; acceptable, clean language and a register for the reader.
- **Ungeniso kwidayari**: "Idayari kukubonisa amava akho buqu"; a review of the day or event; thoughts, told in the first person where it fits; simple, direct language; the tone depends on what is written.
- **Udliwano-ndlebe**: ask the interviewee questions; show the interviewee's strengths and talents; speaker names on the left of the page; a colon after the speaker's name; a blank line between one speaker's words and the next.
- **Ukunika imiyalelo / ukwalathisa**: instructions in a correct order, written to the person being addressed (second person, the imperative); directions use landmarks, distance, direction words (`nyuka`, `yihla`, `thambeka` are listed in the CAPS) and thanks.
- **Ingxelo (esesikweni nengekho sesikweni)**: accurate details of an event; headline, introduction, body (who, what, when, where, how, why), conclusion, recommendations; present tense unless it is about past events; third person; formal, impersonal language.

## Eight lessons proposed (the same set as English HL and Afrikaans FAL, Grade 8), once a reviewer has written the content

| # | Term | Proposed lesson (CAPS name) | What the learner must be able to do | English skeleton to be written in isiXhosa by a first-language teacher |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | Ileta yobuhlobo | write a letter to a friend: address, date, greeting, three short paragraphs, closing and first name | writer's address; date; "Dear friend"-type greeting; "I hope you are well"; news of the week (two sentences); a question to the friend; closing; first name |
| 2 | 1 | Ingxelo yophando | plan and write a short report on a school matter: headline, introduction, findings, conclusion, recommendation | headline; one sentence stating the aim; three findings with numbers (for example, how many learners walk to school); a conclusion; one recommendation |
| 3 | 2 | Itekisi enika imiyalelo; ukwalathisa | write steps in order and directions with landmarks | title; "you need" list of four items; four numbered steps with order words (first, then, next, finally); directions from the school gate to the library with two landmarks and a thank-you |
| 4 | 2 | Ingxoxo yababini nodliwano-ndlebe | set out a dialogue and a written interview with speaker names, colons and a blank line | a two-speaker dialogue of six lines (asking the way); an interview of five questions to a teacher about a hobby |
| 5 | 3 | Ileta eya kumhleli wephepha-ndaba | write a formal letter that states a view | two addresses; date; formal greeting; subject line; opinion in the first paragraph; two reasons; a closing request; formal closing with initials and surname |
| 6 | 4 | Inqaku lephephandaba / lemagazini | write an article with a headline, by-line, lead paragraph and body | headline; by-line; lead (who, what, when, where); two paragraphs of detail; a quotation from a speaker; a closing line |
| 7 | 4 | Ungeniso kwidayari | write an entry with date, events in the past tense, feelings, a plan for tomorrow | date line; opening; three sentences about the day (past); one sentence about a feeling; one sentence about tomorrow |
| 8 | 4 | Ingxelo yokuntathela | report an event as a journalist: third person, past tense, facts and a quotation | headline; who, what, where, when, why; one quotation; the result |

House style, when a reviewer has returned the table: five or six slides a lesson in the same shape as the Afrikaans lessons (rule, worked example, common mistake, one picture, last slide "Check yourself"), at most 130 words a slide, isiXhosa in one style with an English gloss, **12 multiple-choice questions** a lesson (96 in all). The question stems may be English; every isiXhosa answer comes from the returned table.

## What a first-language reviewer must write or check

The reviewer's work is in two kinds: **write** (we have nothing, and will not invent anything) and **check** (existing material in the current Grade 8 lessons).

| # | Item | Reviewer must | Why we cannot do it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Key words per lesson (about 8 each, 64 in all): letter parts, report parts, steps and order words, direction words, interview words, diary words | **write** each word in school spelling with an English gloss | spelling and the school terms (for example the word for "headline", "by-line", "subject line", "step") vary and some are not in the CAPS |
| 2 | Four to six model sentences per lesson | **write**, with the right subject concords, tense markers and (where relevant) honorifics | we do not write isiXhosa sentences |
| 3 | A model text of about 60 words for lessons 1, 2, 5, 6, 7 and 8 (lessons 3 and 4 can be a layout with example lines) | **write** | same |
| 4 | Letter greetings and closings for a friend, and the formal greeting and closing for the editor | **write** and decide: `Mhlobo wam` / `Umhlobo wakho` as the current T1 lesson has them, the formal greeting, the closing, whether initials and surname follow | CAPS gives the layout but not the words; current wording is on the doubts list |
| 5 | Order words and the imperative with two forms (singular and plural), and the direction words | **write**, and confirm `nyuka`, `yihla`, `thambeka` (from the CAPS) and the form used when thanking | concord and mood forms |
| 6 | The dialogue and interview layout: speaker name, colon, new line, blank line | **confirm** that this layout is what Grade 8 teachers set (the current Writing T1 and T2 lessons already show `UAyanda: ...`) | CAPS names the layout; our lessons show it with names |
| 7 | Names after the copula and the speaker label: `UAyanda:`, `NdinguAyanda` | **decide once** whether names are joined or spaced and whether the speaker label takes the initial `U` | the Grade 8 and Grade 9 lessons use the conjunctive form; the same ruling is needed in every lesson |
| 8 | Tense and register in each type: present for the report, past for the diary and the journalistic report, imperative for instructions, formal for the editor | **write** one model sentence for each | tense forms |
| 9 | Two common mistakes a learner makes in each text type (16 in all): wrong concord, spacing of the copula, wrong register, wrong tense | **write** | needs a first-language teacher's classroom experience |
| 10 | The 12 questions per lesson (96 in all) | **write** the isiXhosa answers and distractors: real, plausible isiXhosa, exactly one right; distractors should not be nonsense | distractors must be plausible errors, which only a teacher can judge |
| 11 | The vocabulary of the existing Grade 8 lessons (the "Doubts" lists at the top of each term in `audit/xhosa-g8-teacher-notes.md`) | **check** in the same pass, since the new lessons will reuse some of it (for example `Ndiyathemba ukuba uphilile`, `ethala leencwadi`, `Kubiza malini?`) | the existing lessons have not been checked by a first-language teacher |
| 12 | Terms where usage varies: the word for "letter to the editor" (the CAPS table says `ileta eya kumhleli wephepha-ndaba`; the term table says `ileta ebhalelwe umhleli`), "interview", "headline", "report" | **decide** which form schools use, and give a spelling for each | CAPS uses both forms |

### Return format (what makes the next step quick)

A table per lesson with these columns: `isiXhosa | English | register (formal/informal) | tense | reviewer note`. Every row becomes a line in the lesson and in the doubts file (as for the Afrikaans lessons, where `docs/notes/g8-afrikaans-doubts.md` lists each phrase). A test (as for Afrikaans, `src/curriculum/grade8/afrikaans/transactional.test.ts`) will fail if a lesson shows an isiXhosa phrase that is not in the returned table.
