# Grade 8 Geography gaps: points for a teacher to confirm

Source: DBE CAPS Social Sciences Grades 7-9, Grade 8 Geography Terms 1 to 4 (sp_ss). Branch C adds four lessons to Terms 1 and 2 beside the term lessons that were already live. All time zones use standard time only. No real rainfall or temperature figures are given for named South African places; the lessons are qualitative there and send learners to a weather service table.

## Points that need a teacher's opinion

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Equinox and solstice | Axis tilt 23,5°. The tropics are drawn at 23,5° N and S. | The tilt is 23,4° or 23,5° depending on the source; 23,5° is the usual school value. |
| 2 | Equinox and solstice | Dates given as "about 21 December", "about 21 June", "about 21 March and 23 September". | The real dates move by a day from year to year, so "about". |
| 3 | Equinox and solstice | Johannesburg is taken as 26° S and Cape Town as 34° S, Durban as 30° S in a question. | Rounded to whole degrees for the arithmetic. |
| 4 | Equinox and solstice | "The Earth is a little closer to the Sun in early January" in the common-mistake box. | True (perihelion is in early January) and used only to correct the idea that distance causes the seasons. |
| 5 | Equinox and solstice | The hottest weeks come "some weeks after the solstice". | Seasonal lag; no figure given. |
| 6 | World time | South African Standard Time is UTC+2 all year, with no daylight saving. London is shown as GMT (UTC+0), not British Summer Time. | Standard time only, as agreed; other countries' summer time is not taught here. |
| 7 | World time | Cape Town "about 18° E, about 48 minutes later", Durban "about 31° E, about 4 minutes earlier", from 4 minutes per degree. | Longitudes are rounded; the exact figures differ by a minute or two. |
| 8 | World time | Delhi is UTC+5:30 and shown with a half hour. | Standard time in India is UTC+5:30. |
| 9 | World time | The International Date Line is described as running "near 180°". | It bends around some islands and countries. |
| 10 | Climate factors | Air cools "about 6,5 °C for every 1 000 m" (an average). | The usual average lapse rate; real values vary with moisture and place. |
| 11 | Climate factors | Descriptions of Cape Town, Durban, Johannesburg, Upington, Port Nolloth, Beaufort West, George, Mbombela, Mmabatho and Mthatha are qualitative (for example "most rain in winter", "summer rain"). | CAPS asks for average monthly figures; the lessons send learners to a weather service table rather than quote numbers. Please check each description. |
| 12 | Climate factors | George is described as having "rain throughout the year". | A common description of the Garden Route; the pattern is not perfectly even. |
| 13 | Climate factors | The Agulhas Current is "warm" and the Benguela Current is "cold". | Standard. The lessons do not name the Mozambique Current. |
| 14 | Climate kinds | Ten kinds of climate follow the CAPS list. | CAPS says other atlases may group them differently as long as six or more are used. |
| 15 | Climate kinds | The three example climate graphs (Mediterranean, tropical, desert) use invented values and are labelled "invented values, not a real place". Example A total 580 mm; Example B 2 320 mm with a 1 °C range; Example C 37 mm. | No real place is described by these numbers. |
| 16 | Climate kinds | The latitude strip is labelled "schematic, not to scale" (tropical 0 to 15, deserts 15 to 35, temperate 35 to 60, polar 60 to 90 degrees). | Real boundaries are not straight lines of latitude. |
