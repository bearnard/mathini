# Grade 12 History (Wave A): points for a teacher to confirm

Source: DBE CAPS FET History Grades 10-12, **Afrikaans edition (Geskiedenis)** read as text (the English edition was not reachable). Every lesson source is an **original or paraphrased description written for the lesson**, labelled as such; no real speech, photograph or memoir is quoted.

## Cold War branch (terms 1: origins, China, Vietnam, Angola)

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | All | Names of case-study elements and the order of topics were read from the Afrikaans KABV. | A teacher should confirm against the English CAPS. |
| 2 | Origins | The three interpretations (traditional, revisionist, post-revisionist) are described in general terms, without named historians. | CAPS asks for "different viewpoints" without naming schools; the labels are standard in textbooks. |
| 3 | Origins | "A Marshall Plan speech of 5 June 1947; the aid itself began in 1948." | Standard dates; the plan's passing in Congress was in April 1948. |
| 4 | China | The Sino-Soviet split described through "peaceful coexistence", Mao's wish to lead, and the 1969 border clashes. | A simplified account; historians stress different causes. |
| 5 | China | "China backed North Vietnam against the USA, but the two fought a short border war in 1979." | Standard. CAPS lists Vietnam among China's neighbours. |
| 6 | China | The comment that China was not a superpower by 1976 is given as an argument with strengths and weaknesses, not as a verdict. | CAPS asks "to what extent". |
| 7 | Vietnam | The three phases follow CAPS (1957-65, 1965-69, 1969-75). Events earlier than 1957 are the background. | CAPS wording. |
| 8 | Vietnam | "Four students killed at Kent State, 4 May 1970." | Standard. |
| 9 | Angola | **Cuito Cuanavale is presented as contested** (South African/UNITA and Angolan/Cuban accounts); no lesson text or question names a winner. The agreed point is that the battles raised the cost of the war and the three governments soon negotiated. | Contested; a teacher should confirm the framing. |
| 10 | Angola | "China backed movements opposed to the MPLA for a time, but played a small role." | CAPS lists China among the involved states; the detail of Chinese support is brief and approximate. |
| 11 | Angola | "South Africa invaded in 1975 and later supported UNITA." | Standard; the exact start of South African support is complicated. |
| 12 | Angola | Peace dates: Bicesse Accords May 1991; Savimbi killed February 2002; peace April 2002. | Standard dates. |

## Civil society branch (term 2: Civil Rights, Black Power, women and peace)

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | All | Dates and names (Brown 17 May 1954, Parks arrest 1 Dec 1955, boycott 5 Dec 1955 to 20 Dec 1956, March on Washington 28 Aug 1963, Civil Rights Act 2 Jul 1964, Bloody Sunday 7 Mar 1965, Voting Rights Act 6 Aug 1965, King killed 4 Apr 1968) are standard and asserted in tests from one facts file. | Standard; the teacher should still confirm against the English CAPS. |
| 14 | Civil Rights | The boycott length is given as "over a year" (5 December 1955 to 20 December 1956) instead of a day count. | The usual figure of 381 days depends on how the days are counted; the lesson avoids a precise number. |
| 15 | Black Power | Black Power is presented as several groups that did not agree, with different historians' judgements of the Panthers (community work against armed image and conflict with police). The FBI's action is described in general terms. | Contested; a teacher should confirm the balance. |
| 16 | Black Power | Malcolm X: "left the Nation of Islam in 1964; views on race changed after the pilgrimage to Mecca." | Standard; historians differ on how far his views changed. |
| 17 | Women and peace | "About 20 000 women" at the Union Buildings on 9 August 1956; leaders Lilian Ngoyi and Helen Joseph. | Commonly quoted figure; estimates vary. |
| 18 | Women and peace | Kent State, 4 May 1970, four students killed. | Standard. The lesson does not say that protest ended the war: it says historians disagree. |
| 19 | Women and peace | CAPS Topic 3 lists the women's movement in general terms; the lesson treats second-wave feminism briefly and South African women more fully. | A teacher should confirm the emphasis. |

## Not covered on purpose

- Events before 1949 in China, as CAPS says ("not for examination").
- The Soweto-type detail of South African topics (other branches and Wave B).
