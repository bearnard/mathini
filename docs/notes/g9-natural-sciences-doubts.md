# Grade 9 Natural Sciences, Wave A: Terms 3 and 4 gaps, facts for a teacher to check (bron, 2026-11-17)

Source of scope: CAPS Natural Sciences Senior Phase Gr 7-9, Grade 9 Terms 3 and 4. Plan: audit/wave-a/bron-g9-natural-sciences-plan.md. Questions carry no picture, so every situation is described in words and numbers.

## Branch A: Forces; Safety with electricity (T3)

- **g = 10 N/kg.** The lesson uses g = 10 N/kg, a rounding of 9,8 N/kg, and says so. Every weight question gives g, so a learner who uses 9,8 in class is not caught out. The Moon's gravity is "about one sixth" of the Earth's, so 600 N becomes about 100 N.
- **Magnetic substances.** The CAPS sentence on magnetic substances is garbled in the text ("paper, wood, plastic, iron, brass, aluminium ... including iron, steel, cobalt, nickel"). The lesson teaches iron, steel, cobalt and nickel as the magnetic substances, which is the correct physics, and the question asks "iron and steel". A teacher should confirm this matches the practical the class did.
- **Electrostatics.** Only electrons move; the ruler rubbed with a cloth is shown gaining a negative charge and the cloth a positive charge. Which material gains electrons depends on the pair of materials, so the lesson does not name a general rule.
- **Lightning safety** is given as general advice (stay in a building or a hard-topped car; keep away from open ground, tall trees and water). The CAPS only asks for "safety precautions".
- **Fuse, circuit breaker, earth leakage, 3-pin plug.** Described as in the CAPS. **No wire colours and no fuse ratings** are taught, because the CAPS gives none and South African colour codes changed over the years; a test fails if a wire colour appears. The 10 A and other current values in the examples are teaching values, not recommended ratings.
- **Overload** is taught as in the CAPS: the total current in parallel branches is the sum of the branch currents; if it is more than the rating of the breaker or fuse, the device trips. A total exactly equal to the rating is not treated as overloaded.
- **Energy theft** is described as in the CAPS (illegal connections to the Eskom supply are dangerous and regarded as energy theft).

## Branch B: Electricity generation and the national grid; Cost of electrical power (T3)

- **Power station chain** as in the CAPS: coal is burnt to boil water, steam turns a turbine, the turbine turns a generator. Nuclear: heat from fission boils the water; the rest of the chain is the same.
- **Alternatives** (wind, falling water, sun-heated steam, nuclear fission, waves) are compared in one line each on sustainability and impact (renewable or not, depends on weather, dams flood land, waste is radioactive). These are standard textbook statements, not figures.
- **Koeberg** is named as the nuclear power station (Western Cape), as the CAPS does. **Medupi, Kusile and Majuba** appear as coal stations in one wrong-answer set; they are real coal-fired stations. No output, age or cost figures are given for any station.
- **Grid loss.** 15% of the energy is wasted as heat in power lines and transformers (CAPS). Questions always give the energy sent. "Step down by transformers" is stated as in the CAPS; no detail on alternating current.
- **Overload** of the grid is mentioned as in the CAPS ("power surges and grid overload can disrupt the power supply"); no dates, stages or load-shedding schedules are given.
- **Unit price.** R2,50 per kWh is a teaching value, not a municipal tariff. Every question that needs a price gives it, and the lesson says to find the price for the learner's municipality.
- **Power ratings** (kettle 2 000 W, heater 1 500 W, TV 100 W, 60 W incandescent lamp, 11 W compact fluorescent lamp) are typical teaching values, not measurements of particular products.
- **kWh, cost and prepaid** arithmetic is exact; the helper computes and a test recomputes each key (for example 750 W for 4 h at R2 = 3 kWh = R6; R30 at R2 per kWh on a 1 500 W heater lasts 10 hours).

## Branch C: The Atmosphere (T4)

- **Composition.** The CAPS gives nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%, carbon dioxide "less than 1%" and "other gases, including water vapour (1%)". The lesson shows nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21% and about 1% for carbon dioxide, water vapour and the other gases together (the real figures: argon is about 0,9% and varies with water vapour). The questions only ask for nitrogen and oxygen and the sum.
- **Layer heights** are the CAPS ones: troposphere to about 10 km, stratosphere about 10 to 50 km, mesosphere about 50 to 80 km, thermosphere above 80 km (fading out at about 350 km; the ISS at about 370 km). The scale drawing asks for thicknesses (40 km and 30 km), not heights. Real layer boundaries vary with latitude and season; the lesson says "about".
- **Temperature gradient** in the troposphere is "about 1 °C per 100 m in still air" (CAPS); the questions always give the sea-level temperature and say "in still air". The real average lapse rate is nearer 0,65 °C per 100 m, but the CAPS value is the one examined.
- **The greenhouse effect: CAPS wording versus physics.** The CAPS text says greenhouse gases "trap the ultraviolet radiation which then warms the air". In fact greenhouse gases trap heat (infrared radiation) given out by the warm Earth; it is the ozone layer that absorbs ultraviolet radiation. The lesson teaches "greenhouse gases trap heat" and does not say which radiation, and no question asks it, so a learner who answers with the memo wording is not marked wrong. **A teacher should know about this wording before marking an answer on the topic.**
- **Ozone.** The lesson says ozone in the stratosphere absorbs ultraviolet radiation and that too much ultraviolet harms life, as the CAPS does. It does not go into the ozone hole or chemicals.
- **Effects of global warming** (climate change, rising sea levels, food shortages, mass extinctions) are listed as in the CAPS without figures.
