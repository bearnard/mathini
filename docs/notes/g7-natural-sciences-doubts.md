# Grade 7 Natural Sciences: doubts

Things I could not confirm from the CAPS text (Senior Phase NS Gr 7-9, Gr 7 Term 1 "Variation", Term 4 "Historical development of astronomy") and chose to word carefully. A subject expert should check them.

## Variation (Term 1)
- The class data (30 heights, 21 of 30 tongue rollers) is made up for the lesson; it is labelled as one class. CAPS asks learners to collect their own, so the lesson shows the method (percentage, grouped bar graph) rather than a real survey.
- The lesson says tongue rolling is inherited, because CAPS says so ("some inherited characteristics are height and tongue-rolling"). Genetics research shows tongue rolling is not a simple single-gene trait; I follow CAPS and do not teach genes.
- Species: CAPS says "all people are human (Homo sapiens)". The dog example ("big dog and small dog are one species") is mine and correct for the domestic dog.

## History of astronomy (Term 4)
- 29,5 days (average time between full moons) and 365 days (year) are rounded values. The 354 vs 365 comparison is my illustration of why early calendars needed adjusting.
- isiLimela: the Pleiades are called isiLimela ("the digging star") in isiXhosa and isiZulu, and their return to the dawn sky around June marks the time to prepare the land. This is widely documented but not in CAPS. Please check the wording and the month.
- Southern Cross pointer: "carry the long axis on about 4½ times" is the standard navigation rule of thumb; the picture is a sketch, not to scale, and says so in its description.
- Dates are the CAPS dates: Copernicus 1514, Kepler 1609, Galilei 1610, Newton 1687. CAPS says Galilei "made the first telescope"; the lesson says "built a telescope" because telescopes existed before 1610 and Galilei's contribution was using one to observe planets and moons.
- CAPS says "the Moon and Sun and stars seem to move"; the lesson keeps that wording.

## Periodic Table (Term 2)
- Layout: the picture shows the first 20 elements in the short 8-column form (groups 1, 2, 13 to 18) with the transition elements left out, and says so in its description. CAPS only asks for the first 20 by name and symbol, not atomic numbers.
- Hydrogen is placed in group 1 as in the usual table but is a non-metal; the mistake box says so.
- Boron and silicon are the semi-metals among the first 20. Sources differ on a few borderline elements (for example germanium and arsenic are outside the first 20, so not an issue here).
- "Symbols come from the element's name in other languages too": Na is from Latin natrium and K from kalium. Wording kept simple on purpose.
- Mercury as the liquid metal is CAPS wording; it is not in the first 20.

## National electricity grid (Term 3)
- 60 W and 10 W bulbs are an example I chose to show "energy-saving bulbs". CAPS gives no wattages; no kWh or rand costs are taught (that is Grade 9).
- "South Africa makes most of its electricity from coal": true at the time of writing, but the share changes. No percentage is given. Please check the wording is still right when published.
- Nuclear fuel is listed as non-renewable (correct) because CAPS lists it as a source for the grid.
- Dynamo examples are the CAPS ones: bicycle lights, mine helmets, wind-up torches and radios.
