# Grade 11 Geography gaps: doubts and decisions

CAPS (Grade 11 Geography): Term 1 Droughts and Desertification (8 h) and the skills Aerial Photographs and Orthophoto Maps; Term 2 Fieldwork; Term 3 Frameworks for Development (6 h: factors, Rostow, core and periphery, sustainability, community-based development) and Using Atlases.
- All data are imaginary teaching data (the town of Kopano, a 400 ha farm, a trade balance, a week of weather); no real map, photograph or Stats SA figure is quoted. Pictures are drawn schematics. `geo11.ts` is tested in `geo11.test.ts` and recomputed independently in `g11GeoGaps.test.ts`.
- Drought: the "below 75% of the mean" rule is stated as the rule of the lesson, not as an official definition. The causes listed (high pressure, El Nino, climate change, human water use) are general. Examples: Sahel and Karoo.
- Desertification case studies: the Great Green Wall (planned belt across the Sahel; progress is uneven) and farmer-managed tree regrowth in Niger are named only in one sentence each, with no figures. A teacher may want to confirm the wording.
- Rostow: five stages; the "more than about 10% of national income" investment mark is the usual textbook description of take-off. Criticisms are general (Western path, colonial history, environment, unclear boundaries).
- Core and periphery: Gauteng as the national core and the rural Eastern Cape as periphery is a common textbook example; spread and backwash effects follow Myrdal.
- Community-based development: Grameen Bank (Bangladesh) and the Green Belt Movement (Kenya) named in one sentence each.
- Rule of 70 is used for doubling time as a standard approximation.
- Atlases: G12 has an atlas lesson (thematic maps, isolines, index and degrees and minutes). The Grade 11 lesson is pitched to the Grade 11 CAPS (kinds of atlas maps, projections, alphanumeric and geographic references) and shares only the degrees-and-minutes idea. Africa 30,4 million km² against Greenland 2,17 million km² gives about 14 times (rounded, approximate).
- Aerial and orthophoto: G12 also has an aerial/orthophoto lesson. The Grade 11 lessons concentrate on identifying landforms (using the Term 2 landform names), tone, texture and shadow, photograph scale and ground distance and area, and share only the AerialTypes picture and mapwork.ts helpers.
- Fieldwork is low weight in exams; it is taught through weather instruments and processing a week of imaginary readings.
