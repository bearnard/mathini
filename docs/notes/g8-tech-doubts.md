# Grade 8 Technology: doubts and decisions

## Term 1 (feat/g8-tech-t1)
- Scope follows the Grade 8 CAPS Term 1 text (read from the official PDF). Graphic communication: line types (outline thick/dark, construction thin/feint, hidden detail dashed, centre line chain dash-dot), scaling up and down, dimensions in mm, single-view 2D working drawing, isometric on a grid, double vanishing-point perspective with colour, texture and shading. The drawing skills themselves are practical; the lesson teaches the conventions and the scale arithmetic.
- Scale arithmetic is plain division and multiplication, tested. The isometric picture is computed from a tested projection (x and y at 30°, z vertical); the front, left and top faces are all shown.
- Frame structures: the king-post truss is the CAPS example (rafter, tie beam, king post or queen post, strut, tie). The forces named (rafters and struts in compression; tie beam and king post in tension) are the usual textbook statement for a simple truss under a roof load. Real forces depend on the load and the design, and the lesson says "in this truss". The rafter length uses Pythagoras on half the span and the rise (6 000 mm span, 2 000 mm rise gives about 3 606 mm).
- Failure: the three CAPS ways (fracture from lack of strength, bending or buckling from lack of stiffness, toppling from lack of stability). The toppling angle is the tilt at which the line down from the centre of gravity leaves the base, arctan(half the base ÷ height of the centre of gravity), rounded to one decimal place in the lesson (18,4° for a 20-unit base and 33,7° for a 40-unit base at a height of 30). This is an idealised block, not a real structure.
- Not built: the Mini-PAT (design brief, sketches, model, presentation; 70% of the term), the pylon pictures and the practical worksheet on members under tension and compression.

## Term 2 (feat/g8-tech-t2)
- Scope follows the Grade 8 CAPS Term 2 text: the positive impact of technology (new or improved materials, biodegradable materials), case study 1 (plastic shopping bags and thicker, biodegradable bags that shoppers must buy), case study 2 (recycling waste paper and cardboard into packaging), the development of an opened container, packaging design (the nature of the product decides the design and material), case study 3 (a negative impact and how to counteract it), the forces on materials (tension, compression, bending, torsion, shear), adapting materials (reinforcing concrete, plywood) and metal sections (I-beam, angle iron, T-bar).
- Case studies are described in general terms; no statistics on plastic bags or recycling rates are quoted (the CAPS gives none, and local figures vary). The recycling steps are the usual simple sequence (collect, pulp, clean, press and dry).
- "Biodegradable" is stated as "broken down by micro-organisms". Some biodegradable plastics need industrial conditions to break down; the lesson says so in the bag-charge example.
- The net is the usual cross-shaped development of a box (four side faces in a row, top and bottom on one side). Surface area is computed and tested; the sheet size does not include flaps and the waste is not asked.
- Reinforcing: in a beam loaded in the middle and supported at the ends the bottom is in tension and the top in compression, so the bars go near the bottom. Real reinforced beams also have stirrups and cover; not taught at this level.
- Metal sections: the I-beam comparison is an area comparison (a 100 mm × 100 mm solid bar against an I-beam of the same depth with 10 mm flanges and web: 10 000 mm² and 2 800 mm², 72% less metal). It does not claim equal strength: it only says the shape puts the metal where bending forces are greatest.
- Not built: the Mini-PAT 2 (counteracting a negative technology: design, model, evaluation) and the practical making of packaging.

## Term 3 (feat/g8-tech-t3)
- Scope follows the Grade 8 CAPS Term 3 text. Gears: counter-rotation, the idler (same direction; harder material), bevel gears (axis through 90°), velocity ratio, force ratio (mechanical advantage) from tooth ratios or diameters, and the design targets (force 3 times, speed 4 times). The CAPS says not to use "taking moments about a point", and the lesson does not. Levers (the other half of the CAPS mechanical advantage item) are already taught in "Simple Machines Revision" and are not repeated.
- Definitions used: velocity ratio = output speed ÷ input speed = driver teeth ÷ driven teeth; mechanical advantage = output force ÷ input force = driven teeth ÷ driver teeth. Friction is ignored (an ideal gear pair, so MA × velocity ratio = 1, which is tested). Textbooks differ on the name "velocity ratio" (some define it as the inverse); the lesson states its definition in every worked example and in the Rule.
- Bicycle terms follow the CAPS (master or driver, slave or driven, chain wheel, cogs). "Low gear for hills" is stated for the chain wheel and cog on the same bicycle.
- Systems diagrams: input, process, output, as in the CAPS (gear system with MA 4; a gear train with the driven gear faster than the driver).
- Mining: the provinces named are from the CAPS Mini-PAT context paragraph (gold Gauteng and Free State, coal Mpumalanga and KwaZulu-Natal, platinum Limpopo and North West, iron and copper Northern Cape and Limpopo). No tonnages, prices or depths are quoted. Acid mine drainage and dust are described by cause, effect and solution. The Iron Age furnace description (charcoal, clay pipes) is general; no site is named.
- Gender bias is defined as "an unfair preference" and the lesson says the law now supports equal opportunities; it does not give statistics.
- Not built: the Mini-PAT 3 (a tender for a mine head-gear lifting structure with budget, isometric and working drawings, flow chart) and the practical gear-drawing tasks.

## Term 4 (feat/g8-tech-t4)
- Ohm's law is QUALITATIVE, as the Grade 8 CAPS says ("introduce Ohm's Law (qualitatively - no calculations)"): more cells in series give more voltage, so the current increases if the resistance does not change. A test asserts that no question in the resistors lesson uses V = IR, "calculate", ohms or amperes. The two earlier Grade 8 circuit lessons (Natural Sciences term 3) follow the same rule.
- Cells: the 1,5 V figure is the usual nominal value of a dry cell; a fruit cell gives only "a small voltage" (no figure is given because it depends on the fruit and the metals). Identical cells are assumed; "three cells in parallel last about three times as long" assumes the same load and ignores real-world losses. The advantages and disadvantages follow the CAPS items (series and parallel batteries, solar cells).
- Power stations and the grid: thermal (steam turbines with coal, gas, nuclear or the sun as the heat source), hydroelectric (with pumped storage) and wind, with advantages and disadvantages, then AC, step-up and step-down transformers and the grid stages. No transformer equation, no efficiency figures and no South African production percentages are quoted (they change from year to year).
- Impact and bias: energy for heating, lighting and cooking in rural and informal settlements, illegal connections (ethical and safety issues) and equitable sharing of resources, taken from the CAPS items. The lesson asks for a balanced report and does not take a political position.
- Logic gates: AND (switches in series) and OR (switches in parallel) with exhaustive truth tables (tested over all four input combinations); the panic button system uses OR. The Mini-PAT 4 (design brief, circuit diagram, model, poster) is not built.
- The existing Grade 8 lessons "Electrical Systems and Circuit Safety" and "Logic and PAT Design Work" are CAPS Term 4 content in Term 2 (Wave B list).
