# Grade 8 Technology: doubts and decisions

## Term 1 (feat/g8-tech-t1)
- Scope follows the Grade 8 CAPS Term 1 text (read from the official PDF). Graphic communication: line types (outline thick/dark, construction thin/feint, hidden detail dashed, centre line chain dash-dot), scaling up and down, dimensions in mm, single-view 2D working drawing, isometric on a grid, double vanishing-point perspective with colour, texture and shading. The drawing skills themselves are practical; the lesson teaches the conventions and the scale arithmetic.
- Scale arithmetic is plain division and multiplication, tested. The isometric picture is computed from a tested projection (x and y at 30°, z vertical); the front, left and top faces are all shown.
- Frame structures: the king-post truss is the CAPS example (rafter, tie beam, king post or queen post, strut, tie). The forces named (rafters and struts in compression; tie beam and king post in tension) are the usual textbook statement for a simple truss under a roof load. Real forces depend on the load and the design, and the lesson says "in this truss". The rafter length uses Pythagoras on half the span and the rise (6 000 mm span, 2 000 mm rise gives about 3 606 mm).
- Failure: the three CAPS ways (fracture from lack of strength, bending or buckling from lack of stiffness, toppling from lack of stability). The toppling angle is the tilt at which the line down from the centre of gravity leaves the base, arctan(half the base ÷ height of the centre of gravity), rounded to one decimal place in the lesson (18,4° for a 20-unit base and 33,7° for a 40-unit base at a height of 30). This is an idealised block, not a real structure.
- Not built: the Mini-PAT (design brief, sketches, model, presentation; 70% of the term), the pylon pictures and the practical worksheet on members under tension and compression.
