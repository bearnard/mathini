# Grade 10 Life Orientation: facts to check (teacher review pack)

Source for the topic lists: CAPS FET Life Orientation Grades 10-12, Annual Teaching Plan for Grade 10 (DBE). Everything below is in a lesson or a question bank and
should be read by a Life Orientation teacher before it counts as reviewed. Nothing here was left out of the bank because of doubt; where a fact could not be
confirmed from a primary source it is NOT stated as a number or a date in the lesson.

Status key: OK = taken straight from CAPS, CHECK = a teacher or the owner should confirm, SENSITIVE = health, sexuality or religion content that needs a teacher's eyes.

## Term 1

### Self-awareness, self-esteem and self-development
- OK: the five strategies for building confidence (communicate, finish tasks or projects, take part in community life, make good decisions, affirm others) and respect for differences of race, gender and ability are the CAPS list.
- CHECK: the examples (Sipho, Lerato, Thabo, Zanele) are invented SA names and scenarios, not real people.

### Power, power relations and gender (SENSITIVE)
- OK: the concepts (power, power relations, masculinity, femininity, gender), the differences between a man and a woman (reproduction and roles), stereotypes, gender differences in physical activity, and the effects of gender inequality (sexual abuse, teenage pregnancy, violence, STIs including HIV and AIDS) are the CAPS list.
- CHECK: the wording "sex = biological differences, gender = roles and expectations a society attaches" is the usual school definition, not a CAPS quotation.
- CHECK: Childline is given as "116" (the number the owner and orchestrator gave). Confirm that 116 is the current toll-free Childline number. No other helpline number is printed in a lesson (a test fails on a full phone number). The national GBV Command Centre (0800 428 428) is NOT included until a teacher confirms it is current.
- SENSITIVE: the Sibusiso pressure example avoids graphic detail on purpose.

### Subjects, career fields and study choices
- CHECK: "The National Senior Certificate needs seven subjects: two languages (a Home Language and a First Additional Language), Mathematics or Mathematical Literacy, Life Orientation and three other subjects." Confirm against the current DBE National Policy on the Programme and Promotion Requirements.
- CHECK: "Many engineering degrees need Mathematics (and Physical Sciences)." True in general; each university sets its own admission requirements, so the lesson says "many" and tells the learner to check.
- CHECK: the life domains use the CAPS wording: being (physical, psychological and spiritual), becoming (practical, leisure and growth) and community (social, physical and community). Many textbooks call the third domain "belonging"; the lesson follows CAPS.
- OK: the salary example (gross R15 000, deductions R3 000, net R12 000) is made-up illustration; the numbers come from `gr10Helpers.ts` and are tested. No real tax rates or thresholds are stated.

### Diversity, discrimination and human rights violations
- OK: contexts (race, religion, culture, language, gender, age, rural or urban, xenophobia, human trafficking, HIV and AIDS status) are the CAPS list.
- CHECK: the definitions of xenophobia ("fear or hatred of foreigners") and human trafficking ("moving people by force, fraud or deception to exploit them") are simplified school definitions.
- CHECK: the South African Human Rights Commission and the Commission for Gender Equality are named as organisations that challenge discrimination (both are Chapter 9 institutions of the Constitution).

### The Bill of Rights and international instruments
- CHECK: the Bill of Rights is Chapter 2 of the Constitution of 1996 (confirmed in the Constitution text).
- CHECK: the limitation of rights ("by a law that is reasonable and fair") is the idea of section 36; the lesson does not quote section numbers.
- CHECK: Convention on the Rights of the Child adopted by the UN in 1989 (child = under 18); African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child adopted in 1990 by the Organisation of African Unity (now the African Union); CEDAW adopted by the UN in 1979. Years and wording from the instruments' own texts. The dates South Africa ratified them are NOT stated.
- CHECK: descriptions of the Public Protector ("investigates wrongdoing in government"), Legal Aid South Africa ("legal help for people who cannot pay") and the two commissions are one-line summaries.
- CHECK: Human Rights Day is 21 March; the 16 Days of Activism for No Violence against Women and Children run from 25 November to 10 December.

### Physical Education: fitness and safety
- OK: the four fitness parts and safety in fitness exercises are the CAPS list.
- CHECK: the World Health Organization advice quoted is "an average of at least 60 minutes a day of moderate to vigorous physical activity" for children and teenagers aged 5 to 17 (WHO guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour, 2020).
- CHECK: the warm-up, main activity and cool-down times (10, 30, 10 minutes) are an example, not a prescription.

## Term 2

### Study skills and study methods
- OK: the lists of study skills, study methods, critical, creative and problem-solving skills, the process of assessment (internal and external) and the annual study plan are the CAPS Term 2 list.
- OK: "Life Orientation is the only subject that is not externally assessed", the five internal tasks per grade, and the Grade 10 and 11 marks per term (task out of 80 plus PET out of 20, four terms, 400 in all, each term 25% of the year) are from CAPS section 4 (Programme of assessment, recording and reporting table). The worked year mark (60 + 15, 56 + 14, 64 + 16, 60 + 15 = 300 out of 400 = 75%) is invented; its numbers come from `gr10Helpers.ts` and are tested.
- CHECK: the 25-minute study block with a 5-minute break is the usual "Pomodoro" idea, offered as one example, not a CAPS requirement.
- CHECK: "external assessment is set and marked outside the school" is a short school definition; the Grade 12 NSC examinations are the usual example.

### Social and environmental responsibility
- OK: the list of social issues, the harmful effects on health, the civic skills, youth service and the group project follow the CAPS Term 2 text.
- CHECK: the two definitions (social justice and environmental justice) are simplified school definitions, not quotations.
- CHECK: the links between an issue and a health harm (unsafe water and water-borne disease, hunger and malnutrition, violence and trauma) are general public-health facts and should be read by a teacher.

### Physical Education: games and safety
- CHECK: jukskei, diketo and morabaraba are named as examples of indigenous South African games. The lesson says only that jukskei involves throwing a pin at a target. A teacher should confirm that these are good examples for the learners' region and that nothing about the rules is wrong.
- OK: the safety checks, warm-up, water and sun protection are general and uncontroversial.
- OK: the catch record (4, 5, 7, 8 out of 10) is an invented example.

## Term 3

### Life roles and their responsibilities
- OK: the roles listed (child, student, adult, partner, mother, father, grandparent, breadwinner, employee, employer, leader, follower), their evolving nature, how roles change relationships and the influence of society and culture are the CAPS Term 3 list.
- CHECK: the examples (Thando, Sipho, Lindiwe, Mpho) are invented. The gender-stereotype example about housework should be read by a teacher for tone.

### From adolescence to adulthood (SENSITIVE)
- OK: the physical, emotional and social changes, skin problems and coping with change follow the CAPS list. CAPS says "seed formation"; the lesson says "sperm production".
- CHECK: the skin-care advice (wash gently twice a day, do not squeeze, see a clinic if very sore) is general advice.
- SENSITIVE: no ages for the start of puberty are given on purpose, and nothing graphic is shown.

### Responsible decisions about sexuality and lifestyle (SENSITIVE)
- OK: the behaviour list, the values (respect, abstinence, self-control, privacy, the right to protect yourself, the right to say no, responsibility), the skills and "where to find help" follow the CAPS Term 3 text. Abstinence is taught as a CAPS value.
- CHECK (law): "The age of consent for sex in South Africa is 16" (Criminal Law (Sexual Offences and Related Matters) Amendment Act 32 of 2007, as amended). The lesson does NOT explain the rules for consenting adolescents aged 12 to 15 or the two-year age-gap rule; a teacher should decide whether Grade 10 needs more.
- CHECK (law): "Sex without consent is rape, at any age" (the 2007 Act defines rape as an act of sexual penetration without consent).
- CHECK (law): "A child of 12 or older can ask a clinic for an HIV test or contraceptives" (Children's Act 38 of 2005, section 130 on HIV testing and section 134 on access to contraceptives; section 129 gives children of 12 or older the right to consent to their own medical treatment if mature enough). Wording kept general.
- CHECK (health): "Medicine that helps to prevent HIV works best within 72 hours" after rape (post-exposure prophylaxis; the national guideline says to start as soon as possible and within 72 hours). A nurse or doctor should confirm the wording.
- CHECK: Childline is given as 116 (the number the owner gave). No other helpline number is printed in a lesson.
- CHECK: "Alcohol weakens judgement" and the risk chain are general statements.
- SENSITIVE: the lesson says in two places that the person who abuses is responsible and the person who was hurt is never to blame.

### Diversity of jobs and career fields
- OK: primary (raw materials), secondary (finished products) and tertiary (infrastructure and services), indoor and outdoor settings, designing, assembling, growing, information gathering and instruction, careers in recreation, fitness and sport, salary package, promotion and further study follow the CAPS text.
- CHECK: the example jobs by sector (farming and mining primary, factories and building secondary, teaching and nursing tertiary) are the usual textbook examples.

### Physical Education: recreation and relaxation
- OK: recreation and relaxation, and safety issues, follow the CAPS PE bullets for Term 3.
- CHECK: the breathing example (in for 4 seconds, out for 6, five times) is one common pattern offered as an example; the numbers come from `gr10Helpers.ts`.
- CHECK: "Swim only where there is a lifeguard or an adult watching" is general water-safety advice.

## Term 4

### Job market trends, qualifications and lifelong learning
- OK: the topics (trends and demands, scarce skills, growth and decline, SAQA, the NQF, recognition of prior learning, lifelong learning, formal, informal and non-formal learning) are the CAPS Term 4 list.
- CHECK: the NQF has 10 levels; matric (the National Senior Certificate) is level 4, a bachelor's degree is level 7 and a doctorate is level 10. Confirm against the SAQA level descriptors. The difference "7 − 4 = 3" is computed by `gr10Helpers.ts`.
- CHECK: the lesson names electricians and solar technicians, information technology, health care and skilled trades as often scarce or growing, and routine office and repetitive factory tasks as often declining. These are general trends, not an official list; a teacher should compare with the current DHET list of occupations in high demand before they are relied on.
- CHECK: the three kinds of learning are defined in the usual way (formal: schools, colleges and universities; non-formal: organised outside the formal system, such as workshops; informal: everyday learning).

### Religions, belief systems and fairness in sport (SENSITIVE)
- OK: the list of religions (Judaism, Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, the Baha'i Faith, African religion) and the indigenous belief systems follow the CAPS Term 4 text. The lesson describes and never ranks a belief.
- CHECK (religion): holy books (Christianity: the Bible; Islam: the Qur'an; Judaism: the Torah; Hinduism: the Vedas; Buddhism: the Dhammapada appears only as a wrong answer to a question about Islam); Ramadan fasting from dawn to sunset; zakat (giving to the poor), tzedakah (charity and justice), seva (selfless service) and ubuntu as examples of caring for others. A teacher of each faith, or a Religion Studies teacher, should read these lines.
- CHECK: "many honour a Supreme Being and the ancestors" for African religion is a general statement, not a claim about every community.
- CHECK (sport): bias examples (unequal funding, facilities and coverage; exclusion by race in the past), the redress actions, and the four unfair practices (doping, match-fixing, subjective umpiring, maladministration) are the CAPS list with one-line definitions.
- CHECK (law): freedom of religion and belief is a right in the Bill of Rights (no section number is quoted).

### Physical Education: traditional and non-traditional sport
- OK: the CAPS bullet is "activities that promote skills in traditional and/or non-traditional sports; safety issues".
- CHECK: the school-context definition (traditional = widely played in the community or school; non-traditional = less common there) is the lesson's own working definition, because CAPS does not define the terms.
- CHECK (health): "after a knock to the head, stop and get medical help if you feel dizzy, confused or have a headache; return only when a doctor says it is safe" is general concussion advice and should be read by a teacher.
- OK: the archery practice log (4, 5, 6, 7 out of 10) is an invented example; the improvement comes from `gr10Helpers.ts`.
