# Grade 9 English HL transactional texts (Wave A): points for a teacher to confirm

Source: DBE CAPS English Home Language, Senior Phase Grades 7-9 (Grade 9 teaching plans and the text-type table). Every model text is an **original model written for the lesson** with invented names, places and organisations.

## Term 2 branch (diary entry, constitution and policy)

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both | Placement in term 2 follows the CAPS writing task ("Diary entry/constitution/policy"). | CAPS plan tables extract poorly; confirm the term against the printed CAPS. |
| 2 | Diary | "Dear Diary," is shown as optional; the date at the top is stressed. | CAPS does not say; widespread practice. |
| 3 | Constitution | The model parts (name, aims, membership, meetings, amendments, adoption date, signature) extend the CAPS list (name, membership, terms of reference, date of adoption, signature, "etc."). The aims clause is taught as the terms of reference. | CAPS says "etc."; a teacher may prefer other headings. |
| 4 | Constitution | "Must" for a duty and "may" for a permission. Older legal texts use "shall". | Style choice; plain-language convention. |
| 5 | Policy | The policy is taught as one short model (purpose, rule, exception, adoption). | CAPS names "policy" with the constitution but gives no separate format. |

## Term 3 branch (will and testament, CV and covering letter)

| # | Lesson | What | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Will | CAPS lists the will in term 2 (reading) and term 3 (reading and writing). One lesson is placed in term 3, where the writing task is. | A teacher may want the term 2 reading as a separate short lesson. |
| 7 | Will | Execution: signed by the testator and by witnesses in the presence of the testator and of each other; "a witness should not be a person who will receive something". No question depends on this, and the lesson tells learners to see a lawyer for a real will. | The exact rules (the Wills Act 7 of 1953: the number and age of witnesses, the age of a testator, initialling pages) are not taught. A lawyer or teacher should confirm the wording. |
| 8 | Will | The model will names an executor, beneficiaries and "the rest"; clause headings follow the CAPS list (date, estate, beneficiaries, language, execution). | CAPS gives a list of elements, not a format. |
| 9 | CV | The CV has the CAPS parts (personal details, formal qualifications, work experience, referees) and a "post applied for" line. | Textbook layouts differ (some add an objective or interests). |
| 10 | Covering letter | "Yours faithfully" after "Dear Sir or Madam", "Yours sincerely" after a name. | Standard SA/UK convention; some teachers also accept "Dear Sir/Madam". |
| 12 | Covering letter | The closing follows the CAPS formal letter description: the formal closing, then the writer's surname and initials ("Mahlangu L."), and the subject line is underlined. | Many textbooks sign with the full name; CAPS words it as surname and initials. |
| 11 | Both | Telephone numbers and e-mail addresses are placeholders (082 000 0000, example.com). | Chosen so that no real person is contacted. |
