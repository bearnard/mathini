# Grade 12 Life Sciences, Wave A: facts to check and sensitive content (bron, 2026-11-17)

Source of scope: CAPS FET Life Sciences Gr 10-12, Grade 12 content (Term 2 Genetics and Inheritance rows; Term 4 Human Evolution continued). Plan: audit/wave-a/bron-g12-life-sciences-plan.md.

## Genetic Engineering (T2) and DNA Fingerprinting, Paternity and mtDNA (T2)

- **Stated from general knowledge, not from the CAPS text** (the CAPS gives only the topic names): recombinant DNA steps (restriction enzyme, plasmid, DNA ligase, bacterium; human insulin made in bacteria); Bt maize and cotton (gene from Bacillus thuringiensis, protein toxic to certain insect pests); golden rice (beta-carotene, converted to vitamin A); South Africa grows GM maize, soya and cotton; stem cells (embryonic vs adult, bone marrow transplant for leukaemia); Dolly the sheep (born 1996, nuclear transfer from an adult udder cell into an egg cell with its nucleus removed, no fertilisation). A teacher should confirm these match the textbook the learner uses.
- **Concerns about GMOs** are stated as concerns "people raise" (gene flow to wild relatives, allergies, seed dependence), not as established harm. "Eating GM food does not rewrite your DNA" is a standard correction.
- **Ethics of embryonic stem cells and cloning** are stated as views people hold on both sides. No position is taken.
- **DNA profile bands** in the lesson and questions are band POSITIONS (1 = nearest the wells) invented for teaching, not real data. The paternity rule used is the standard one: every child band must come from the mother or the father, so the bands the mother lacks must be the father's. A real test compares many more bands and gives a probability; the lesson says only that a man who lacks a required band is excluded and that a man who has them all cannot be excluded.
- **Forensics:** a match is described as "the sample could have come from the suspect", never as proof of guilt.
- **mtDNA:** maternal inheritance, the egg providing the mitochondria, change only by mutation, use for tracing maternal lineages and for the African origin of modern humans (CAPS Term 3: "Genetic evidence: mitochondrial DNA"). "Mitochondrial Eve" is not used.
- **Identical twins** are mentioned as the exception to "every person has a different profile".

## The Cradle of Humankind (T4), approximate ages and who found what

The CAPS names the sites and scientists but gives **no ages**. The lesson states ages only as "about", from standard South African textbook values, because learners are examined on them. A teacher should confirm the figures against the textbook the learner uses; several are revised when new dating is published.

| Item | Value in the lesson | Where it comes from / caution |
|---|---|---|
| Taung Child (Australopithecus africanus, Taung, North West) | about 2,5 million years; Raymond Dart, 1924 | Dart's 1924 description is certain. The age is the usual textbook figure; the Taung site is hard to date. |
| "Mrs Ples" (Sterkfontein) | about 2,1 million years; Robert Broom (with John Robinson), 1947 | Discovery year and finder are certain. The age is the usual textbook figure and differs a little between sources. |
| "Little Foot" (Sterkfontein, Australopithecus) | about 3,67 million years; Ron Clarke | The 3,67 million figure comes from a 2015 dating study and is disputed by other scientists (some give a younger age). Shown as "about" only. |
| Kromdraai (Paranthropus robustus) | Robert Broom, 1938 | Certain. No age given. |
| Swartkrans | C.K. Brain (Paranthropus robustus and early Homo) | Certain that Brain led the work; no age given. |
| Malapa (Australopithecus sediba) | about 2 million years; Lee Berger, 2008 | Discovery year certain; the age is the usual rounded figure (a little under 2 million). |
| Brain sizes in the picture | about 450 cm3 (A. africanus), about 900 cm3 (Homo erectus), about 1 350 cm3 (Homo sapiens) | Rounded textbook values for the trend; the ratio 3 is computed from them in a test. |

- **Tobias, Keyser and the other sites in the CAPS list** (Plovers Lake, Gladysvale, Makapansgat): the lesson names Tobias (studied the Sterkfontein fossils and early Homo) and lists Gladysvale as a Cradle site, and gives no detail for the others, so that nothing unchecked is taught. A teacher may want to add Makapansgat (Dart) and Keyser (Gladysvale, Drimolen).
- **Florisbad, Border Cave, Blombos** (the later sites in the CAPS list) are described by province and what they show (early modern human fossils; engraved ochre and shell beads showing symbolic thinking) with **no ages**, because the published ages differ by source.
- **Dating:** the lesson states the standard point (carbon-14 only for young fossils, older fossils by dating the rock around them with other radioactive isotopes), which CAPS refers back to Grade 10.
- **Cradle of Humankind** is described as a World Heritage Site in Gauteng and North West, north-west of Johannesburg.

## Alternatives to Evolution (T4): SENSITIVE, teacher review before release to a class

- **Scope.** CAPS names four explanations (Creationism, Intelligent Design, Literalism, Theistic evolution) and asks for "research and discussion to share information about different explanations: cultural or religious". The lesson defines each term and compares them. It does **not** argue for or against any of them.
- **Framing.** Evolution by natural selection is described as "a scientific theory built on evidence that can be tested". The alternatives are described as what people believe or hold. No belief is called wrong, false or unscientific; a test fails if a ranking word appears in the lesson, and no question asks which explanation is right (every question asks what a term means or how they differ).
- **Definitions used.** Creationism: the belief that life was created by a creator such as God. Intelligent Design: the claim that some complex features of life point to an intelligent designer (it does not name the designer). Literalism: reading a holy text, such as the Genesis creation story, as an exact account of events. Theistic evolution: the belief that God used evolution, accepting the scientific evidence for it. A teacher or a person of the relevant faith should confirm these are fair.
- **Cultural example.** One Zulu tradition (uNkulunkulu came out of a bed of reeds, the uthlanga) is used as an example of a cultural explanation. It is the standard account, but it should be checked by a Zulu-speaking teacher, and other communities' stories could be added.
- **Not covered.** Other religions' accounts beyond the example above, and the debate about teaching creationism in schools.
