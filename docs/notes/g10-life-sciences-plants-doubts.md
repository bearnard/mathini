# Grade 10 Life Sciences, plants: doubts and decisions

CAPS (Grade 10 Term 2, Support and Transport Systems in Plants, 3 weeks): anatomy of dicotyledonous plants (root and stem: distribution of tissues and the cells of the tissues), secondary growth and annual rings (age and climate), transpiration (water loss and leaf structure; temperature, light intensity, wind, humidity; wilting and guttation), and uptake and transport of water, minerals and manufactured food (translocation).
- Two lessons beside "Plant Tissues and Organs", which keeps tissue types. The pictures are drawn plans (root, stem, annual rings) with labels; they are schematic and not to a real scale.
- Annual rings: ten imaginary widths (4,2; 3,8; 1,5; 4,0; 5,0; 2,0; 3,6; 4,4; 1,2; 3,8 mm; total 33,5 mm, mean 3,35 mm); ring 5 widest, ring 9 narrowest. The link between ring width and rainfall is stated as a general guide.
- Potometer data (12, 24, 30 and 6 mm in 6 minutes) are imaginary; rates 2, 4, 5 and 1 mm/min, +100%, +150% and -50% are computed in code and recomputed in the test.
- Water entry is explained by osmosis (water potential gradient) and mineral entry by active transport. The cohesion-tension detail is limited to "loss of water pulls water up the xylem".
- The stem root and bundle layout follows the usual dicot plan (root: xylem star in the centre with phloem between the arms; stem: ring of bundles, xylem inside and phloem outside, cambium between).
- Teacher check: the statement that guttation happens at night when the soil is wet and the air is humid; and that mature xylem vessels are dead and hollow while sieve tubes are living.
