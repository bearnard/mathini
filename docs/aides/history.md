# Learning Aides — History (Gr 6–12)

## Subject Overview

History in the SA CAPS curriculum spans Grades 6–12 — from local community history and historical enquiry
in the Intermediate Phase, through industrialisation, colonialism, WWI, and WWII in the Senior Phase, to
the FET's deep dives into the Russian Revolution, Cold War, Civil Rights, African independence, and
Apartheid. Assessment is built around **chronological thinking, source analysis, causation essays, and
interpretation of multiple perspectives**.

Digital tools have a natural fit here: history is fundamentally about visualising time, place, and viewpoint —
all of which are uniquely expressible in an interactive browser environment.

| Grade Band | Phase | Key Topics |
|---|---|---|

## Recommended Aide Types

### 1. Interactive Timeline Builder
**Priority: 🔴 High**

A drag-and-drop or click-to-build timeline where learners place events, add annotations, and draw
cause–effect arrows between nodes. Supports zoom from decade to century scale.

**Rationale:** Chronological thinking is the first and most consistently assessed historical skill across all
CAPS phases (Gr6 "sequencing events" through FET "chronology prompts"). A digital timeline lets learners
construct understanding rather than read pre-built charts.

**Component ideas:**
- Zoom-scale slider (decade ↔ century ↔ world history span)
- Colour-coded event categories: political / social / economic / military
- Cause–effect linking arrows between event nodes
- Pre-built teacher-designed timelines per topic (WWII, Apartheid, Russian Revolution)
- Learner-generated annotation submissions for teacher review
- SA-specific thread: apartheid legislation timeline, Soweto 1976 chain of events

---

### 2. Primary Source Analysis Scaffold
**Priority: 🔴 High**

A structured document viewer presenting historical sources (text, photograph, cartoon, map extract) with
a step-by-step provenance–evidence–evaluation framework alongside.

**Rationale:** Source-based questions are assessed every term from Gr6 upward, and are explicitly named
in every CAPS ATP reviewed. Learners consistently struggle with evaluating reliability and identifying bias.
A digital scaffold mirrors the CAPS skill framework and models expert practice.

**Component ideas:**
- Split-view: source on left, guided prompts on right ("Who? When? Why? What does it show? Can we trust it?")
- Highlight/underline tool with colour codes (fact / opinion / bias / emotion)
- Zoom and rotate for map or image sources
- Worked examples with expert annotations visible on demand
- Photo, cartoon, poster, and map source types supported
- SA-specific sources: struggle-era photographs, Apartheid propaganda, newspaper front pages

---

### 3. Causation & Consequence Diagram Tool
**Priority: 🔴 High**

A visual planning tool for constructing causation chains. Learners categorise causes as
short-/long-term or social/economic/political, link them, and use the diagram as an essay plan.

**Rationale:** Causation essays are the dominant FET essay type. Learners know individual facts but
cannot organise multi-causal arguments. Visual structuring before writing is an evidence-backed
pre-writing strategy.

**Component ideas:**
- Pre-seeded cause cards for each FET topic (rise of Nazism, Cold War origins)
- Short-term / long-term axis with drag placement
- Social / economic / political filter colours
- Export as essay-plan scaffold with introduction prompt
- Teacher-designed vs. learner-generated diagram modes

---

### 4. Historical Map Explorer
**Priority: 🟡 Medium**

An overlay map viewer showing how borders, empires, and settlement patterns changed across time,
with a time-scrubber to animate transitions.

**Rationale:** Topics like the Scramble for Africa, WWI fronts, Cold War spheres of influence, and
Apartheid homelands demand spatial understanding. Animated map overlays make geopolitical change
viscerally clear.

**Component ideas:**
- Time-scrubber to animate border changes per topic
- SA-specific overlays: colonial maps → Union → apartheid homelands → post-1994 provinces
- Clickable regions with explanatory pop-ups and source links
- Side-by-side comparison mode (past / present)

---

### 5. Multiple Interpretations Viewer
**Priority: 🟡 Medium**

A "perspectives gallery" showing two or three historical accounts of the same event from different
viewpoints, with a structured comparison framework.

**Rationale:** Comparing interpretations is explicitly assessed from Gr9 upward. The tool makes
historiography concrete for secondary learners who struggle with the concept that history involves
contested narratives.

**Component ideas:**
- Side-by-side source panels with guided comparison prompts
- "Agree / Disagree / Partially agree" response with evidence-citation tool
- Structured writing frame for comparative paragraphs
- SA examples: Anglo-Boer War accounts, Soweto 1976, Cold War viewpoints

---

### 6. Essay Planner & Writing Coach
**Priority: 🟡 Medium**

A guided essay-planning tool enforcing the History essay conventions (context → argument →
evidence → conclusion), with prompts calibrated to FET-level expectations.

**Rationale:** FET History carries a heavy essay burden (25% SBA / 75% exam). Many learners
have the content but lack the structural discipline. A stage-gated planner builds this habit.

**Component ideas:**
- Introduction scaffold: context → clear argument → roadmap
- Paragraph builder with evidence-counter ("Have you cited at least 2 sources?")
- Drag-to-reorder paragraph structure
- Peer-review checklist aligned to CAPS History marking rubric

## Inspirational Examples

- **TimelineJS** (Northwestern Knight Lab) — embeddable timeline builder used by journalists; inspires
  rich-media event cards in the timeline component
- **Stanford History Education Group "Reading Like a Historian"** — step-by-step source-analysis
  lessons that directly mirror the CAPS source-based assessment framework
- **Google Arts & Culture** — high-resolution primary-source archives and historical image collections
- **Sutori** — collaborative story-based timeline platform used in secondary History classes globally
- **Mindset Learn (SA)** — SA-produced History video lessons aligned to CAPS FET topics
